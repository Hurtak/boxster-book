# PDK transmission

Instead of the standard six-speed manual, the 2010 Boxster 2.9 could be ordered with the optional **Porsche Doppelkupplung**, or **PDK**, which swaps the clutch pedal and manual selection for electronically controlled hydraulics. Inside are seven forward ratios, two wet clutch packs and two routes by which the engine can drive the final drive. The driver can let it choose gears or request changes at the lever or steering wheel.[^catalogue]

PDK's trick is preparation. While one gear drives the car, the next can already be engaged on the other input shaft, and a shift hands the drive from one shaft to the other. **Selecting a gear** and **closing a clutch** are separate steps; that distinction explains far more than the number of gears.

## Two clutches and two input shafts

The [manual-transmission chapter](../08-manual-transmission-and-clutch/index.mdx) explains how a clutch passes torque through friction, and how the dual-mass flywheel works. PDK also takes its drive through a dual-mass flywheel, but splits the clutch into two independently controlled packs. Each holds several discs, giving plenty of friction surface in a small space. Hydraulic pressure sets each pack's clamping force, and oil carries away the heat of slip.[^clutches]

The two input shafts share a centreline: shaft 1 runs inside hollow shaft 2, like a rod through a tube, and each can turn at its own speed. Clutch **K1** drives shaft 1, which carries the odd gears, **1, 3, 5 and 7**, and reverse. Clutch **K2** drives shaft 2, which carries the even gears, **2, 4 and 6**. Both sets of gears drive the same final drive.[^paths]

The clutch packs are nested too, and this is easy to get wrong: the **outer clutch pack, K1, drives the inner input shaft**, while the inner pack, K2, drives the hollow outer shaft. "Inner" on its own is therefore ambiguous, so this chapter always says whether it means a pack or a shaft.

<Figure src={illustration3} alt={"End view of concentric input shafts: inner shaft 1 serves odd gears and reverse; hollow shaft 2 serves even gears. Outer clutch K1 connects to inner shaft 1, while inner clutch K2 connects to hollow shaft 2."}>

The input shafts end-on: inner shaft 1 carries the odd gears and reverse, hollow shaft 2 the even gears. Note the crossover, with outer clutch K1 driving the inner shaft and inner clutch K2 the outer one. In this original schematic the clutches are named in the labels rather than drawn, and dimensions and bearings are omitted.

</Figure>

Hydraulic actuators select the gears through synchronisers and sliding sleeves, using the same [speed-matching principle as the manual gearbox](../08-manual-transmission-and-clutch/index.mdx#constant-mesh-and-synchronisers). PDK automates the selection and controls the clutches separately.[^selection]

## What happens during a shift

When the car accelerates in first, K1 is closed and first is driving. Second can already be engaged on the even shaft with K2 open: its gear train turns with the moving gearbox, but the open clutch leaves it free to run at a different speed from the engine. "Ready" means a fully engaged gear behind a disconnected clutch.

When the controller calls for second, it reduces K1's grip while increasing K2's. Controlled slip during this handover lets the engine speed settle towards what second needs. Once K2 carries the drive, K1 opens, and the unloaded odd shaft can release first and prepare third. A downshift reverses the direction of the ratio change, with engine-speed coordination helping the incoming gear take over smoothly.[^overlap]

At a given road speed, two gears would need two different engine speeds, so the gearbox cannot lock both clutches solid while both gears are engaged. Preselection works because the waiting gear sits behind an open clutch; the overlap works because a friction clutch can be engaged gradually.

<Diagram id="pdk-power-path" poster={illustration1} alt={"First gear drives through closed clutch K1 while second is preselected behind open clutch K2. Both gear paths feed the final drive, but only the closed-clutch path carries engine drive in this steady state."} />

*Step through an upshift or downshift and watch the two clutches. The last stage readies a plausible next gear; it does not reproduce the gearbox's prediction software.*

Preselection is a prediction. If demand changes suddenly, a different gear may be needed and the selectors must move again, so a change to the waiting gear and an unexpected jump of several gears need not feel the same. Nor does unbroken power flow mean constant wheel torque: changing ratio changes the torque multiplication while engine and clutches are coordinated. The [differential chapter](../10-differential-driveshafts-and-mounts/index.mdx) follows the drive on to the wheels.

## The selector and steering-wheel controls

The centre lever offers the familiar automatic positions. Forward-gear requests are electronic and hydraulic, but a mechanical cable also runs from the selector to the gearbox: in P, the selector mechanism engages a catch with the parking-lock gear on the pinion shaft, independently of the clutches.[^operation]

| Position | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **P** | A parking lock mechanically holds the transmission output. It works separately from the clutches. |
| **R** | Selects reverse, using the odd/reverse clutch path. |
| **N** | Neutral disconnects the engine's drive. It does not hold the car. |
| **D** | The controller chooses forward gears automatically. |
| **M** | The driver chooses forward gears with the lever or the steering-wheel controls. |

The original PDK sports steering wheel has two push/pull switches, so either hand can change either way: push forwards for up, pull back for down. The January 2010 UK catalogue also offers a three-spoke paddle wheel: pull the **right paddle for up** and the **left for down**. The centre lever follows Porsche's period arrangement, forwards for up and back for down.[^wheel]

A steering-wheel request can temporarily override D. In M, the controller still checks that the requested gear suits road and engine speed and refuses changes outside the permitted range; it also changes down by itself to stop the engine speed falling too low, and kickdown still works. In manual mode the driver chooses the gear, but the clutches always operate automatically.[^operation]

## Hydraulics, feedback and heat

PDK's **mechatronic control system** combines electronic decisions with hydraulic force. A hydraulic block in the sump area sends fluid to the clutches and gear-selection actuators, and electrically operated valves turn the controller's commands into changes of pressure or flow. Pressure sets how firmly each clutch is clamped; actuator movement decides which sleeve is engaged.

The factory parts drawing shows this unit above a separate bolted oil sump. The housing joins the engine, carries the gears and final drive, and has left and right support brackets. The final drive's short and long output flanges, which connect to the rear driveshafts, are different parts, even if a functional diagram draws them as a matching pair.[^hydraulics]

<Figure src={pdkHydraulicParts} alt="PDK transmission housing above a separated hydraulic control block and bolted oil sump, with output flanges beside the housing." width="520px">

In the optional PDK, the hydraulic unit (11) sits above the removable oil sump (7), below the larger housing that carries the transmission and final drive. This valve-and-actuator hardware is separate from the electronic controller in the luggage-compartment area. Factory drawing: Porsche AG, [PET 320-005, PDF p. 121](https://www.porsche-preparation.com/downloaded/pet-porsche/boxster-2009-2012-type-987.pdf#page=121).

</Figure>

Sensors close the loop. Input-speed sensors show how each clutch path is turning, selector-position sensors show where the selection mechanism has moved, and pressure and temperature readings help operate and protect the clutches. The electronic PDK controller is a separate unit in the rear-right luggage-compartment area, linked to other controllers over CAN. It receives driver demand and vehicle information, including accelerator and brake signals. "Mechatronic" describes the parts working together; the electronics need not sit inside the hydraulic block.[^control]

<Figure src={illustration5} alt={"PDK control and oil functions: an electronic controller commands hydraulic clutch and selector operation; clutch heat reaches an oil-to-coolant exchanger, while separate gear oil lubricates the gears and final drive and loses heat through the housing."}>

The controller commands hydraulic clutch and selector operation. Clutch heat goes to an oil-to-coolant exchanger, while the separate gear oil lubricates the gears and final drive and loses heat through the housing. In this original functional diagram, the arrows distinguish commands, feedback and heat flow, not hose routing or the complete hydraulic circuit.

</Figure>

PDK has **two separate oils**. A special hydraulic fluid operates and cools the clutches and works the gear selection; gear oil lubricates the gears and final drive. Porsche's technical introduction says plainly that they must not be mixed. An oil-to-coolant exchanger outside the gearbox, with oil pipes to the gearbox and separate coolant hoses, removes heat from the clutch and hydraulic fluid, while the gear oil sheds heat through the housing.[^oil]

As with the [manual clutch](../08-manual-transmission-and-clutch/index.mdx#the-flywheel-and-clutch-make-a-controllable-connection), slip makes heat, which the oil supply and exchanger handle when moving off, manoeuvring and shifting. The handbook tells the driver to hold the car on a slope with the brakes.[^temperature]

## Why seventh is a cruising gear

Porsche calls first to sixth the performance gears and says top speed comes in **sixth**. Seventh is taller, lowering engine speed at a given road speed but also multiplying torque less, leaving less to overcome resistance. The highest gear need not be the fastest.[^catalogue]

Using Porsche's rounded ratios of **0.88:1 for sixth and 0.62:1 for seventh**, seventh needs about **70% of sixth's engine speed** at the same road speed, with the clutches fully engaged. The [manual ratio explorer](../08-manual-transmission-and-clutch/index.mdx#ratios-exchange-speed-for-torque) explains the calculation.[^ratios]

## Options on the 2010 2.9

PDK is option **250**. **Sport Chrono 639**, or **Sport Chrono Plus 640 with PCM**, adds control choices, not engine power. Sport changes the accelerator response and changes how PDK shifts. With PDK, Sport Plus adds a more performance-focused shift strategy and Launch Control; the period handbook says that seventh is not used in Sport Plus.[^chrono]

Launch Control coordinates the engine, clutch take-up and permitted wheel slip for a standing start. It heavily loads the engaged components and is neither an extra gear nor a power upgrade; Porsche's handbook restricts it to controlled conditions and warns of reduced component life. The catalogue's acceleration figures show why the equipment matters: for the 2.9, Porsche quotes **5.8 seconds to 100 km/h with PDK**, or **5.6 seconds using Sport Plus from the optional Chrono package**. The full manual and PDK comparison is in [the specification tables](../03-the-2010-boxster-2-9-specification-and-options/index.mdx#the-reference-specification).[^performance]

The optional paddle wheel is **840**. PASM and the mechanical differential are separate options, covered under [suspension](../11-suspension-and-wheel-geometry/index.mdx) and [drivetrain](../10-differential-driveshafts-and-mounts/index.mdx).

## Changes in other 2.9 model years

PDK replaced the Tiptronic S automatic at the 2009 facelift. The January 2010 UK catalogue documents the optional paddle wheel. Later ratios, and the limits of the annual UK evidence, are collected in the [annual matrix](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#annual-equipment-matrix).[^years]

## The S, Spyder and Black Edition

The ordinary S also offers the seven-speed PDK. Porsche's introduction lists the same forward ratios for both cars, though that does not make every assembly or fitting detail interchangeable. The S's extra torque changes what goes into the gearbox, not the odd-and-even principle.[^ratios]

The Spyder also offers a manual or optional PDK. Its January 2010 UK brochure quotes **5.0 seconds to 100 km/h with PDK**, or **4.8 seconds with Sport Plus and the optional Sport Chrono**. Those figures belong to the lighter, more powerful 3.4-litre Spyder.[^spyder]

The March 2011 UK price list makes the SportDesign steering wheel standard on the Black Edition, with paddles when the optional PDK is fitted; PDK and Sport Chrono Plus remain options. That is a dated specification for one edition, not evidence that ordinary 2.9 PDK cars had paddles as standard.[^black]

## Identification and ageing

Porsche's catalogue identifies the 2.9's PDK as **CG200**; the S and Spyder use **CG220**. That distinction still matters even where a drawing or a later replacement part is shared.

Service records should say which of the [two separate oils](#hydraulics-feedback-and-heat) was changed; "gearbox oil changed" alone leaves the question open, so match the work to the gearbox fitted.

Clutch surfaces, seals, bearings, hydraulic valves, sensors, wiring and the dual-mass flywheel all affect behaviour. For a harsh change, delayed engagement, leak or warning, note the temperature and operating conditions. Porsche documents several warning and reduced-operation states; the applicable owner's manual gives their exact responses.[^temperature]

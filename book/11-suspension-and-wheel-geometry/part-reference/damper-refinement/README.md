## What changed in the teaching drawing

- Removed the fictitious external hydraulic loop from both dampers. PASM's electrical cable is kept separate from hydraulic paths and enters the hollow rod at the top.
- Replaced the squat rectangles with a long tube, threaded stem, narrower polished rod, broad bowl-shaped spring seat, welded bracket, rod guide/seal, inner working tube, reservoir annulus, piston and bottom valve.
- Main-body envelope is **44 × 306 SVG units** (about **7:1** length/diameter), spring-seat width **124** (about **2.8 body diameters**). These are photo-guided drawing proportions, not millimetre dimensions. Perspective and rod extension differ between the two photographs, so they do not establish exact stroke, overall length or operating position. No catalogue compressed/extended length is used as a total-strut dimension.
- Valve details are shown in a separate enlargement, not by fattening the whole damper. Blue arrows represent one rebound example (rod extending; upper chamber to lower chamber). The PASM bypass is an additional internal route alongside the main piston valves. Its aperture, armature, disc stacks, spring details and concealed ports remain schematic.
- Passive valving is preset, not constant-force. Normal and Sport remain adaptive ranges; no one-to-one mode/valve-open or mode/valve-closed mapping is implied.
- Coil spring, upper mount, bellows and bump stop are omitted from these bare-damper sections; the PET figure elsewhere in the chapter shows the complete strut. The PASM chassis has its own matched springs and fixed nominal height reduction; pressing the mode button does not vary spring rate or static height.

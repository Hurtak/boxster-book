# Chapter 04: body structure and packaging evidence

Checked 10 November 2026. Baseline is the 2010 European/UK 987.2 Boxster 2.9. This chapter owns body/load-path principles, the separate luggage/roof/engine spaces and body airflow context. Chapter 14 owns roof mechanisms and drains; chapter 07 owns fluid circuits. Research was carried out directly in primary Porsche publications, including archived original documents, with the limitations below.

## January 2010 UK ordinary brochure

Porsche, *The Boxster*, edition **01/10**, colophon **WSLB1101000125**, ©2010:

https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf

Independently opened with the web tool. Existing local PDF `/tmp/boxster-ch05/brochure-2010.pdf` was used for text inspection, original JPEG extraction and full-spread rendering. Printed/PDF numbering is not interchangeable.

- **Printed 66–67, PDF 31:** front longitudinal/transverse deformation structure; side reinforcements, windscreen frame and fixed rollover bars. Factory material-zone illustration distinguishes sheet steel, tailored blanks, high-strength and super high-strength categories, austenitic stainless steel and aluminium. The chapter gives no steel-grade numbers, material percentages, weld counts, crash-test ratings or numerical stiffness. The brochure's promotional safety adjectives are not reproduced as quantified performance claims.
- **Printed 78–79, PDF 37:** two carpeted luggage compartments, **150 l front/130 l rear**, rear capacity maintained with hood down, **aluminium luggage lids**, service hatch for engine-oil and coolant filling. Extracted photograph on p.79 is the red S, explicitly labelled as comparison in the manuscript. The front boot's deep well and rear boot's different shape are visible in the source spread. Combined volume is simple addition, not one uninterrupted cavity.
- **Printed 80–81, PDF 38:** ordinary folding roof stores compactly without loss of luggage storage; hardtop is optional, aluminium, **23 kg**, with heated rear glass. Hardtop wording is on **p.81**, not p.80. Render inspected.
- **Printed 126–127, PDF 59:** VDA luggage capacities agree. The body/aerodynamic discussion does not reuse a generic cD=0.29 for every transmission: the technical table differentiates manual/PDK. No numerical drag coefficient is needed in this chapter.

Photograph and factory illustration were compared with complete rendered spreads, not identified from image numbers alone. See `images/credits.md` for extraction and rights notes.

## Porsche training: inherited 987 structural/airflow concept

Porsche AfterSales Training, *General Servicing & Repair – Sports Cars*, **P001**, ©2007 Porsche Cars North America; front matter says Porsche AG rest-of-world English material was adapted for PCNA:

https://souzaweb.com/caymanregister/downloads/54580-General_Porsche_Repair_%28P001_-_Book_5-07%29.pdf

Web tool identified the full primary PDF but could not fetch its size (18 MB); downloaded directly to `/tmp/boxster-ch04/general-porsche-repair.pdf` and text-extracted. Publication identity inspected. This is **pre-facelift 987 evidence**, used for generation architecture and mechanism, not an assertion that each 2010 material detail remained unchanged.

- **Printed 5.3, PDF 243:** aerodynamic design includes styling, cooling-air guidance, underbody panels and add-on parts. Rear-side air-opening design balances engine-compartment flow/intake supply against lift and resistance.
- **Printed 5.4, PDF 244:** structure development identifies A-pillar/sill joint regions, bulkhead crossmember, tailored blanks and upper load-path connections. The chapter intentionally omits exact steel claims (including the source's boron-steel statement), numerical stiffness and percentage comparisons with 986. This avoids treating a 2007 training page as a 2010 metallurgy map. The SVG is a conceptual connection diagram, not a factory load-path map.
- **Printed 2.29, PDF 117:** left rear-side opening routes engine induction air towards air cleaner, with water-separation arrangements; right rear-side opening supplies engine-compartment ventilation, with scavenging blower drawing air in. The chapter uses the destinations and general sides. It omits earlier airflow-sensor details and water-separator geometry, which need separate facelift verification.
- **Printed 5.13, PDF 253:** confirms 987 front lid aluminium and 130 l rear luggage even hood open. Superseded for baseline capacities/materials by the 2010 UK brochure, not used to transfer old audio/navigation packaging to the reference car.

General mechanics explanations (torsion versus bending, closed/deep sections, joint continuity, load restraint and separate lift/drag concepts) are author explanations. No stiffness, strength, CFD result or crash performance has been calculated for this car. Front cooling outlet wording deliberately identifies a broad lower/front region rather than a single precise exit or a measured streamline.

## Owner's manual: front radiators, spoiler and panel condition

Porsche, *Boxster, Boxster S Owner's Manual*, MY2010 North American edition **WKD 987 021 10, 5/09**:

https://billswebspace.com/Owners_Manual_Boxster_PCNA.pdf

Opened full primary PDF with web tool; existing local `/tmp/boxster-ch14/owner.pdf` and `.txt` used for cross-checks. North American manual used for mechanical operation, not UK standard equipment.

- **Printed 3, PDF 5:** damaged/missing spoilers and underside panels affect driving behaviour.
- **Printed 67–68, PDF 69–70:** ordinary automatic spoiler extends approx **120 km/h**, retracts approx **80 km/h**; manual console control; failure warning and rear-axle lift significance. The manuscript explains separated switching thresholds as hysteresis without assigning an undocumented control delay. No road-speed test is proposed.
- **Printed 76, PDF 78:** radiators/fans at front and separate engine-compartment blower. This corroborates the airflow destinations. No claim that the side vent is a radiator is made.
- **Printed 94, PDF 96; 197–202, PDF 199–204:** rear service flap/fluid filler access corroborates brochure. No coolant-opening or engine-work sequence is taught.

## Spyder: different upper body, not just trim

Porsche, *The Boxster Spyder*, UK **01/10**, printed **16–21**, PDF **7–9**:

https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/brochures2/93/2010%20987%20Boxster%20Spyder.pdf

Opened primary PDF with web tool. Printed p.17 identifies fixed spoiler and reduced lift; p.18–19 aluminium doors and passive-safety components; p.20–21 manual sunshield/weather-protector roof and forward rear-boot stowage. The chapter does not repeat the roof operation or speed limit already owned by chapter 14.

Porsche UK launch material reproduced by The Auto Channel, **25 March 2010**, *The Definitive Porsche Roadster – The New Boxster Spyder*:

https://www.theautochannel.com/news/2010/03/25/470682.html

Web search found the source; normal web open returned 429. Full article downloaded directly to `/tmp/boxster-ch04/spyder-press-release.html` and read. Reading/UK dateline, UK launch price/Experience Centre detail and manufacturer-style release establish the reproduced UK launch account. Its explicit single-piece rear-cover description, aluminium rear lid and aluminium doors supply the rear-material claim absent from the short brochure. Weight-saving itemisation was read but not repeated or summed; no claim that the ordinary boot lid had been steel is made. The archival mirror does not retain a separate named author/byline; this is recorded rather than implying an original live Porsche URL survived. **Provenance then corroborated:** AUSmotive, 26 March 2010, https://www.ausmotive.com/2010/03/26/porsche-boxster-spyder-image-gallery.html, was opened in full with the web tool. Its introduction explicitly identifies the following release as press material from **Porsche UK**. The same titled release below the introduction states the aluminium single-piece rear lid and doors. The manuscript now cites this explicitly attributed reproduction, rather than relying on the unattributed Auto Channel mirror. No technical claims are taken from the host's editorial introduction.

Porsche Engineering, *Magazine 1/2010*, **“Hats off! Boxster Spyder – The development of a Derivative”**, printed **19–22**, especially **20**:

https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/Porsche_engineering/PE-01.2010.pdf#page=20

Opened full original PDF with web tool. Rear lid area roughly **1.5 m²**, paint/final assembly challenge and early simulation/tooling development. This source corroborates the large lid as an engineered derivative component; it does **not** independently state its aluminium material in the inspected text. The chapter keeps the two evidentiary roles separate.

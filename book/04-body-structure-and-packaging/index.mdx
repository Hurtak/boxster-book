# Body structure and packaging

The Boxster's body has to locate the suspension, support the occupants and carry loads from one end of the car to the other, while housing luggage, the engine and a folding roof in a small space. The hard part is the large opening above the seats: a roadster must remain a coherent structure with its roof packed away. [The opening chapter](../01-the-2010-boxster-2-9-in-the-987-2-family/index.mdx#a-roadster-arranged-around-its-engine) shows where the major components sit; this one looks at how the body connects them.

## A connected shell around an open cabin

The 2010 Boxster is a **monocoque**: its load-carrying shell is the structure of the car, with no separate ladder chassis underneath. Porsche's specification describes a galvanised steel bodyshell. The front and rear luggage lids are aluminium.[^shell]

A flat sheet bends easily, but folded into a deep section, joined to another sheet or tied to a crossmember it resists force much better. Shape, thickness, material and joints all count, so a large outer panel need not be the most important structural member, while a reinforcement hidden under the trim ties together several heavily loaded areas.

The **sills** run below the door openings, each with an inner reinforcement behind its outer panel. The formed floor, central tunnel and transverse members tie the two sides together, with a seat-support frame on each side of the tunnel. Ahead of the occupants, the bulkhead and windscreen frame join the front structure to the cabin; behind them, the rear bulkhead closes the cabin off from the engine. Together they give loads several routes around the open centre. Porsche's original 987 training material picks out the sill and A-pillar joints, a bulkhead crossmember and an additional upper load path as areas it developed.[^structure]

</Figure>

When one front wheel rises over a bump before the other, the suspension tries to twist the front of the body relative to the rear. That twisting is **torsion**. Loading both ends while the middle is supported also bends the body. The better the shell resists both, the more consistently the suspension mountings stay where they belong, so that more of the movement is taken by the springs, dampers and bushes and less by the body.

A fixed roof would help by closing a deep box above the floor. The Boxster's hood has to fold, so the lower shell and its joints must do that work with the cabin open. Raising the hood keeps the weather out but does not turn the car into a fixed-roof coupé. Nor does fitting the removable hardtop give the roadster the structure of a Cayman.

### Stiffness and occupant protection have different aims

In everyday driving the shell should hold its mounting points in a fixed relationship. In a serious impact, chosen regions must deform while the passenger compartment copes with the loads that reach it. Meeting both depends on the whole assembly, not on using the strongest steel for every panel.

Porsche describes longitudinal and transverse members in the front deformation zone, rigid side structures, door reinforcements, a reinforced windscreen frame and fixed rollover bars behind the seats. Its material illustration distinguishes several categories of steel, and aluminium. At each end, the visible bumper cover is a separate part from the transverse bumper carrier behind it and the carrier's connections to the body.[^protection] Belts, airbags, seats and their mountings complete the protection; they are covered in [occupant protection and system integration](../18-occupant-protection-and-system-integration/index.mdx).

## Three different spaces behind the seats

Behind the seats of an ordinary Boxster are three separate spaces: the engine compartment, the roof compartment and the rear luggage compartment. In side view they overlap, so a panel that looks like part of one continuous rear deck may cover a different opening with its own mechanism.

The engine sits low behind the cabin, under a removable engine cover with a perimeter seal, which is itself hidden beneath trim. A smaller cover behind the trim on the cabin's rear wall gives a second way into the engine area. The folded hood has its own space behind the occupants, under the painted convertible-top compartment cover, which moves aside to let the hood fold. Further back, the rear luggage lid opens onto the boot and the fluid filler area.[^engine-access]

Mounting the engine low leaves useful space above and behind it, but the surrounding body and roof mechanism limit the directions from which a technician can reach it. Depending on the job, access is through one of the covers or from underneath. The fillers in the boot allow routine oil and coolant checks without exposing the whole engine.

Because the rear wall separates people from machinery, any cover removed during servicing should go back with its proper fasteners, seals and insulation. A cover can fill the hole yet fail to keep out noise or weather. Loose trim also makes noises of its own, which have nothing to do with a worn suspension joint or an engine fault.

[Roof, glass, seals and drainage](../14-roof-glass-seals-and-drainage/index.mdx#the-cover-makes-room-for-the-folding-frame) explains how the roof moves and drains. Here it matters only that its stowage space, with room reserved for the folding frame, is part of the body package.

## Two boots, with different shapes

Porsche quotes **150 litres at the front and 130 litres at the rear**, the rear figure applying with the ordinary hood lowered. Both luggage lids are aluminium. Each has two hinges, a perimeter seal and an upper lock part that meets the latch on the body. Two gas struts hold up the front lid; the ordinary rear lid has one. A small service hatch in the rear boot, with its own frame and sealing parts in the boot lining, exposes the oil and coolant filler points.[^luggage]

<Figure src={illustration4} alt={"A red Boxster S with the front and rear luggage lids raised together, showing that the folded roof and two luggage openings occupy separate regions."}>

Boxster S with both luggage lids raised. The 2.9 shares this two-boot arrangement, with the folded roof in its own space between them. Photo: Porsche AG, *The Boxster* (UK, 01/10), p. 79. [Source](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=37).

That adds up to 280 litres, but not one 280-litre space. A rigid case has to pass through one opening and fit that compartment's depth, width and length, so two smaller bags can use the space better than one large suitcase of the same nominal volume. The front boot is a relatively deep well; the rear has a different, broader shape.

The boot floor and lining mark out the usable space; the hidden recesses beyond them are not meant to be filled. Luggage also has mass, so its restraints and the car's permitted weights still apply when there is room for more. In the cabin, loose objects can be thrown towards the occupants under braking.

## Air around and through the body

The body handles air in two ways. Some should flow around the car with little resistance and a controlled spread of pressure. Some must flow **through** it to cool components or feed the engine. That air needs a destination and an exit, so the size of an intake alone says little about the flow it delivers.

The radiators sit at the front, most of the car's length from the engine; coolant carries the heat forward to them. Air enters through the front openings, passes through the radiators and leaves below and around the front end. The side openings by the engine have other jobs. Porsche's 987 training material says the left one leads to the air cleaner and the right one ventilates the engine compartment.[^air] Left and right mean the car's sides when facing forward, whichever side the steering wheel is on.

<Figure src={illustration5} alt={"Functional flow map separating front radiator airflow, left-side combustion-air intake and right-side engine-compartment ventilation."}>

Three separate air streams: through the front radiators, into the left-side intake that supplies combustion air, and through the right-side opening that ventilates the engine compartment. In this original functional map, arrows show where each stream goes, not measured streamlines or exact duct routes.

</Figure>

Keeping those streams apart explains the underbody. Its panels give air a surface to flow over while leaving chosen openings for cooling and servicing. The parts catalogue lists front, centre, left and right centre, and rear covers, with their brackets, fasteners and heat insulation, plus a transverse tunnel strut. The covers, the strut and the structural floor each do a different job. Porsche's original 987 training material treats underbody panelling and cooling-air guidance as part of the aerodynamic design.[^underbody] A completely sealed underside would conflict with other requirements, but a haphazard set of missing panels also departs from the design. The fluid circuits belong to [lubrication, cooling and exhaust](../07-lubrication-cooling-and-exhaust/index.mdx).

The ordinary car's rear spoiler is a moving part of that airflow. An electric mechanism raises a carrier holding the painted upper part; the catalogue also lists seals and a drainage hose around the mechanism. In automatic mode the spoiler rises at about **120 km/h** (75 mph) and retracts at about **80 km/h** (50 mph). Porsche credits it with reducing rear-axle lift and improving high-speed stability.[^spoiler] Having two thresholds stops it cycling up and down when speed hovers around one switching point. It can also be raised manually at a standstill, so a photograph with the spoiler up says little about how fast the car was going.

Lift and drag are separate effects of air pressure on the car. Less lift helps keep the tyres loaded, but it does not mean the car produces net downforce, and a drag coefficient says nothing about the load on either axle. Nor can axle lift or spoiler downforce be judged from the car's appearance.

## Options on the 2010 2.9

Exterior choices also included **model-designation deletion, 498**, and the **SportDesign styling package, XAL**. Both change appearance, not the shell's load paths. Paint and interior colours are collected in the [colour/trim atlas](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#colours-and-trim).[^body-options]

The optional hardtop fits on top of the existing roadster shell. [Roof equipment](../14-roof-glass-seals-and-drainage/index.mdx#choices-for-the-2010-2.9) covers its construction, attachment and sealing.[^hardtop]

## Changes in other 2.9 model years

The facelift revised the front and rear styling but kept the earlier 987's broad structural concept. Porsche's 2009 introduction describes essentially the same bending and torsional rigidity, and the same side-impact technology, as the 2005 model.[^facelift-body] Year-by-year UK body revisions remain unverified; the [annual matrix](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#annual-equipment-matrix) records them separately from the inherited structure.

## The S, Spyder and Black Edition

The ordinary S has the same two luggage compartments and powered roof. Its different engine, cooling and chassis do not turn its rear luggage lid into an engine cover. The Black Edition also keeps the conventional powered hood, and its special finish and equipment are no sign of a Spyder-style rear body.[^black]

The Spyder's upper body is very different. Its aluminium doors, large one-piece aluminium rear lid and manually fitted lightweight hood are all part of one weight-saving programme, and its fixed rear spoiler and reshaped deck change the airflow too.[^spyder] Since the ordinary car's luggage lids are already aluminium, the Spyder's major difference at the rear is the shape and extent of its lid, combined with a different roof system.

Porsche's development account gives that lid an area of roughly 1.5 m² and describes the challenge of making and assembling it.[^spyder-development] A panel that large has to hold its shape, locate consistently and line up with its neighbours. Sharing a model family therefore does not make the upper body panels interchangeable as a simple styling conversion.

## Identification and ageing

Judge the body by its connections as well as its paint. Uneven panel gaps can come from lid adjustment, hinges, seals or an earlier repair; they are a reason to look further, not a structural diagnosis in themselves. Consider the sills, suspension mounting areas, seams and any repaired joints together.

Underneath, check that the panels and air guides are present and securely fastened: Porsche states that damaged or missing aerodynamic panels affect driving behaviour.[^condition] At the rear deck, tell the boot seal from the roof compartment's drainage before tracing a water leak. Check the spoiler using the handbook's stationary controls and fault guidance, not by experimenting at road speed. The most useful record combines the body variant, its repair history and the parts now fitted.

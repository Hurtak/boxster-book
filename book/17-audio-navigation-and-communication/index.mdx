# Audio, navigation and communication

The Boxster’s audio system has three layers. A source supplies the programme, the head unit selects and controls it, and an amplifier drives the loudspeakers. Telephone equipment adds a microphone and a link to the mobile network.

On the 2010 UK 2.9, the standard head unit is the **CDR-30** radio/CD player. Optional **Porsche Communication Management**, **PCM 3.0**, replaces it as the control centre and brings the UK catalogue’s navigation package. The speaker packages, Sound Package Plus and BOSE, are separate choices available with either unit, so identify the head unit and the speakers separately.[^uk-selection]

## From a signal to moving air

A radio aerial picks up a very small signal carrying the broadcast; the tuner selects a station and recovers its sound. A CD drive reads encoded information from the disc and rebuilds the music. A player connected through AUX has already decoded the music and sends a varying analogue voltage for the left and right channels.

The head unit picks one source and applies the listener’s settings. The amplifier then supplies the power to move the speakers. In a moving-coil speaker, current through a coil creates a magnetic force that drives the cone backwards and forwards, pushing and pulling the air. A tweeter’s small, light diaphragm handles high frequencies; a larger bass driver moves more air at low frequencies. A stereo programme has two channels however many speakers each one feeds.

The music signal controls a much larger flow of electrical energy, which the speaker cone turns into pressure variations in the air. The car’s power supply and data network are explained in [Lighting, electrical systems and security](../16-lighting-electrical-systems-and-security/index.mdx).

</Figure>

The standard system has two dashboard tweeters and a woofer in each door, driven by two output stages rated at 25 W each. In Porsche’s parts drawing the tweeters clip into the dashboard, while each door bass driver sits in a housing secured by four bolts. The drawing also shows a radio aerial amplifier connected to the CDR-30; it handles the received signal, not the power sent to the speakers.[^cdr-training]

## The standard CDR-30

<Figure src={illustration3} alt={"The CDR-30’s monochrome screen and surrounding physical controls in Porsche’s period photograph."}>

CDR-30 shown with the optional integrated six-disc changer. Photo: Porsche AG, *The Boxster* (UK, 01/10), p. 82. [Source](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=39).

</Figure>

The CDR-30 is a radio and MP3-compatible CD player with a 5-inch monochrome display and no touchscreen: hard keys, soft keys beside the display and a rotary control do the work. Its FM dual tuner with RDS diversity improves reception by working with alternative received signals. It stores 30 stations, and its speed-dependent volume adjusts the level with road speed to compensate for changing listening conditions.[^cdr-training]

The optional changer holds six discs inside the dashboard unit. Which disc formats play depends on the drive, not the speakers.[^uk-cdr]

## PCM 3.0 and navigation

PCM brings more equipment into the same console space. Porsche’s technical introduction explains that the navigation module, telephone module and optional changer, previously separate installations, are built into the PCM assembly. There are fewer boxes, but each function still depends on what was ordered.[^pcm-training]

<Figure src={illustration4} alt={"The PCM colour touchscreen and NAVI/MAP keys in Porsche’s period photograph."}>

Optional PCM with navigation; the speaker package is a separate choice. Photo: Porsche AG, *The Boxster* (UK, 01/10), p. 83. [Source](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=39).

</Figure>

The UK unit has a 6.5-inch colour touchscreen, physical function keys and a rotary push control. Its maps live on an internal hard drive, so they do not occupy the disc slot during guidance, and the screen can show the map and the next manoeuvre together. GPS tells the system where the car is; the stored road network supplies the routes. A map update revises that database.[^uk-pcm]

In an older car, the receiver may locate the car perfectly while an old map knows nothing of a newer junction. PCM is not a telephone subscription, and choosing it does not automatically add every communication option.

## Sound Package Plus and BOSE

**Sound Package Plus**, option **490**, is an analogue amplified upgrade. Porsche’s training material calls it fully active: a separate amplifier drives the channels instead of the head unit’s output stages. The UK brochure specifies seven speakers and 185 W in total, in the two dashboard tweeter positions, a dashboard centre position, and a midrange and a bass position in each door. The bass drivers differ from the standard ones, and the system is tuned to the cabin; the source menus are unchanged.[^sound-plus]

**BOSE**, option **680**, uses eleven speakers in the Boxster, including a centre speaker and an active subwoofer, with a seven-channel digital amplifier rated at 385 W. Three speakers sit in the dashboard, four in the doors and four in the enclosure behind the occupants. That rear enclosure contains two bass and two mid/high-range drivers and is catalogued as one replacement assembly, not one individual speaker. BOSE also has its own dashboard and door driver variants.[^bose-hardware] Porsche’s PCM architecture drawing separates an analogue amplifier connection from a digital-amplifier path labelled **MOST**, the multimedia data connection in this design. The speaker-driving current still comes from the amplifier.[^pcm-architecture]

In the January 2010 UK specification, AUX needs the optional interface, USB/iPod control needs PCM and its interface, and the DVD 5.1 example needs PCM and BOSE; an unavailable combination stops the path and names the missing equipment. Processing does not change the number of channels originally recorded. ## Music players and telephones

The optional universal audio interface, **870**, sits in the centre-console storage compartment. In the January 2010 UK catalogue, the CDR-30 version provides **AUX**; the PCM version provides AUX, USB and a period iPod connection, with compatible devices operated through PCM.[^uk-interface] AUX carries only sound, so you choose tracks on the player. USB and the iPod connection also pass supported information and control between device and PCM. Connector, cable, device generation and software all have to match; a USB-shaped socket does not promise universal compatibility.

“Bluetooth” names a wireless link; the profile decides what crosses it. **Mobile phone preparation**, **619**, or **618** with the mounting arrangement, provides hands-free calling through the Hands-Free Profile, HFP. The phone stays connected to the cellular network, while the car supplies the speech interface and basic call control. Over Bluetooth alone the phone uses its own aerial; a compatible aerial-connected cradle can change that. Phone preparation was offered with the CDR-30 as well as PCM.[^hfp]

The optional **PCM telephone module**, **666**, puts a GSM telephone in the car. It uses a SIM inserted directly or a compatible **SIM Access Profile (SAP)** link, together with the car’s radio and aerial. In HFP mode the phone keeps its cellular role, and the optional handset, **669**, cannot handle HFP calls. The [reference table](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#audio-capabilities) separates these original capabilities from compatibility with today’s networks and phones.[^telephone]

<Figure src={illustration5} alt={"Three telephone arrangements distinguish the device that actually connects to the mobile network: the mobile phone in HFP mode without an aerial-connected cradle, or the PCM telephone module with a direct SIM or compatible SAP connection."}>

Which device talks to the mobile network? In HFP mode without an aerial-connected cradle, the phone does. With the PCM telephone module, the module does, using a SIM inserted directly or a phone’s SIM over SAP.

</Figure>

Pairing only sets up the Bluetooth link. A call still needs compatible telephone operation and a network, and pairing does not imply music streaming. The 2010 UK sources described here are the advertised radio, disc and wired player connections; Apple CarPlay and Android Auto were not original functions. If a car has them now, record the retrofit and how it connects to the existing amplifier. Today’s installation and the original order are separate evidence.

## Options on the 2010 2.9

PCM with navigation is **P23**; Sound Package Plus **490** and BOSE **680** work with either head unit. The integrated changers are **692** for the CDR-30 and **693** for PCM. PCM-only options include the telephone module **666**, voice control **671** and the electronic logbook **641**. Phone preparation and the audio interface are separate selections, with the capabilities described above.[^uk-selection]

Sport Chrono **Plus**, **640**, adds PCM performance-display and personal-memory functions, as shown in the [integration matrix](../18-occupant-protection-and-system-integration/index.mdx#options-on-the-2010-2.9). Ordinary music and navigation do not need it.[^chrono]

## Changes in other 2.9 model years

The launch catalogue already lists the CDR-30, PCM, both sound upgrades and the different CDR and PCM external-audio interfaces.[^launch] The [annual matrix](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#annual-equipment-matrix) records the annual evidence; installed software and device compatibility must be checked car by car.

## The S, Spyder and Black Edition

The UK S shares the standard CDR-30 and the main entertainment options.[^uk-selection]

The Spyder starts without a radio. Its January 2010 UK brochure offers the CDR-30 (**695**) and PCM with navigation, but makes **either dependent on Sound Package Plus**.[^spyder]

The March 2011 UK Black Edition price list makes PCM with navigation, Sound Package Plus, the AUX/USB/iPod interface and mobile phone preparation standard. Sport Chrono Plus remains optional.[^black]

## Identification and ageing

Start with the face of the head unit, then the console sockets and the build specification. The CDR-30’s monochrome screen and PCM’s colour touchscreen are easy to tell apart, but a PHONE key alone does not show which telephone hardware is fitted. Speaker grilles or a badge are clues; the amplifier identification and the option record are better evidence of the sound package.

Treat a faulty installation as a chain. If one source is silent, look at its medium, cable, interface or settings; if all are silent, check the shared power, controls and output path. A retrofit can keep the original speakers while changing how sources are selected, converted and controlled, so record each layer separately from the original specification.

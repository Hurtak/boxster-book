# Factory parts references: occupant protection

Checked 10 November 2026 for the **2010 European/UK Boxster 987.2 2.9**, with standard six-speed manual and optional seven-speed PDK. These references support installed assembly identity and attachment relationships. The chapter's period UK brochure and technical publications remain the authority for original equipment and operation.

The source's generic PR key is not infallible in this combined catalogue: for example, the plate-specific airbag list uses PR460 for a sports steering wheel while the generic key reuses 460 for a wheel design. Use the component plate's own description, dated UK documentation and build evidence together. PR250 means PDK; PR480 means six-speed manual. Neither gearbox changes the six-bag ordinary-model arrangement established by the January 2010 UK brochure.

## What these drawings establish

- The rear hoops belong to one joined fixed assembly, distinct from the covers and neighbouring soft-top/drainage parts. Upper guides/retractor supports and the separate belt latches are visible. The period UK brochure establishes steel construction and upper belt anchorage.
- Front, seat-thorax and door-head modules are different assemblies. The door module's long pack and separate cylindrical inflator are visible; the standard/comfort-seat drawing separates backrest frame, carrier, module and fasteners. The sports-bucket list provides its own side-airbag parts.
- The restraint controller/sensors, steering contact unit and dedicated wire sets are physical parts separate from the bag modules. Their presence is not proof of an exact wiring topology or deployment policy.
- The passenger front module is supported by the dashboard retaining structure and an airbag reinforcement plate. The explorer shows an indicative bracket, rather than presenting a decorative dashboard surface as the load-carrying mount.

## Limits and operating sources

PET is a later service-parts snapshot covering multiple years, variants and markets. It does not prove the exact parts originally fitted to one 2010 UK car, a component's material unless named, hidden anchor coordinates, force limits, inflator gas chemistry, bag fold pattern, deployment trajectory, trigger thresholds, control timing, structural load rating or a crash outcome. No installation or restraint-service procedure is derived from these pages.

Original equipment and the fixed steel frame/rollover, seat-thorax/door-head and child-seat distinctions remain supported by Porsche, *The Boxster*, UK **01/10**, printed **66–67** ([PDF 31](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=31)). Sport Chrono/Plus, PASM and PCM dependencies remain supported by that brochure's printed **54–57, 107**, not by the PET key. Porsche's MY2010 owner manual and 2009 technical introduction support the control and diagnostic roles with the market qualifications in [chapter research](../research.md). Generic belt-limiter/inflator operating principles are separately sourced there.

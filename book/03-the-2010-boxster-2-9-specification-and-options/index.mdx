# The 2010 Boxster 2.9 specification and options

Every 2010 European Boxster 2.9 has the same published engine output, with either the standard six-speed manual or the optional seven-speed PDK. Around that common base, buyers could combine wheels, suspension, seats, lighting and communication systems in very different ways.

This chapter is the quick reference for the 2.9, taken from Porsche's January 2010 UK brochure. It describes what the catalogue offered; a car's own factory record shows which options it received.[^scope]

The mpg figures use Imperial gallons. Acceleration is timed over **0–100 km/h**, roughly 0–62 mph, so it is not the same test as 0–60 mph. Consumption and CO₂ are period **NEDC laboratory figures**, for comparing cars rather than predicting a journey; Porsche notes that they apply to standard equipment and that options can affect consumption and performance.[^technical]

## What the gearbox changes

With the manual, the driver uses a clutch pedal and selects each of six forward gears. PDK is a dual-clutch transmission with seven forward gears, changed automatically or on the driver's command. Its top gear is for economical cruising: Porsche states that the car reaches its maximum speed in sixth, which is why the extra gear brings no higher top speed.[^transmission]

PDK's quicker acceleration comes from the same 188 kW engine. The ordinary PDK car is quoted at **5.8 seconds**; the **5.6-second** figure needs Sport Chrono's Sport Plus function.[^chrono]

## Dimensions, luggage and weight

| Published dimension or capacity | Reference 2.9 |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 4,342 mm |
| Width, body | 1,801 mm |
| Width, including exterior mirrors | 1,952 mm |
| Height | 1,292 mm |
| Wheelbase | 2,415 mm |
| Front/rear luggage volume, VDA | 150 / 130 litres |
| Fuel tank capacity | Approximately 64 litres |

For a narrow garage or gateway, use the width including mirrors; the body width is a smaller envelope. The wheelbase is the distance between the axle centres, not the length of the cabin or boots. The published height is for the catalogue car, and PASM lowers the suspension by 10 mm.[^dimensions]

The two luggage figures are for separate compartments, both usable with the roof down. Their shapes and access are covered in [body structure and packaging](../04-body-structure-and-packaging/index.mdx#two-boots-with-different-shapes).[^luggage]

The manual car weighs 1,335 kg DIN or 1,410 kg EC; the 75 kg difference is the EC allowance of **68 kg for a driver and 7 kg for luggage**. PDK adds 30 kg on either basis. These are catalogue comparisons, not weighbridge readings of a loaded, optioned car.[^technical]

## Options that depend on each other

Some options can only be ordered together. Sport Chrono Plus requires PCM, and Launch Control requires both PDK and Sport Chrono; PASM is a separate choice. The [complete function matrix](../18-occupant-protection-and-system-integration/index.mdx#options-on-the-2010-2.9) sets out the dependencies.[^chrono]

The locking differential's January 2010 entry requires **18- or 19-inch wheels**, so it cannot simply be added to a car on the standard 17s. The August 2008 launch catalogue had planned it for **May 2009 at the earliest, with manual transmission**. The January 2010 edition drops both the date and the transmission condition, but that omission alone does not prove it could be ordered with PDK, or show when anything changed. [The differential chapter](../10-differential-driveshafts-and-mounts/index.mdx#changes-in-other-2.9-model-years) compares the two editions.[^differential]

Seat ventilation has several prerequisites, set out in [cabin, seating and climate](../15-cabin-seating-and-climate/index.mdx#options-on-the-2010-2.9), and BOSE takes up the storage-box space on the rear bulkhead. Read the full equipment description, not just the code.[^comfort]

## Reading an individual car's record

Establish the car's identity first, then its equipment. Porsche's contemporary handbook describes a vehicle-data label in the Maintenance booklet, listing the VIN, type, engine and transmission codes, paint and interior identification, and optional equipment. That handbook is the North American edition, so check UK availability against the UK catalogue.

Porsche's PET spare-parts catalogue uses a second set of identifiers. Its 2010 rows give the 2.9 engine as **A120**, the manual gearbox as **G8710** and PDK as **CG200**, and its equipment key uses **480** for the six-speed manual and **250** for PDK. These identify assemblies or production equipment; a UK sales code such as **P23** for PCM/navigation belongs to a different list. Match the assembly type and equipment conditions before comparing part numbers. The saved catalogue is a 2018 replacement-parts snapshot, so it cannot show the original UK option price or order date.[^record]

<Figure src={visualvehicledatafieldssvg} alt="An anonymised example distinguishes VIN, type, engine and gearbox, paint and trim, and equipment fields.">

An illustrative field guide, with identity and option values omitted. It is not a reproduction of an individual car’s sticker. Porsche’s handbook lists these five groups.[^record]

</Figure>

For a UK car, check that the VIN on the documents matches the car, then copy the data label carefully. Keep leading zeros and letters: **P75**, **250** and **XLF** are different kinds of identifier. Look each up in a source for the right model, market and date, because a generic decoder may give a plausible name while missing a package condition or a change between generations.

Keep three columns: **documented factory equipment**, **equipment physically present** and **later work recorded on invoices**. The original order or VIN-linked specification fills the first, inspection the second, and invoices explain the gaps. A replacement wheel, radio or steering wheel changes a car's appearance, not its original order; equally, an option missing from a short advertisement may still have been fitted at the factory.

If the label is incomplete or missing, look for the original order documents, ask Porsche for an authenticated VIN-linked specification, and record anything unresolved as such. This chapter's codes are a guide only: they assign no undocumented equipment to any car, and not every production identifier was a paid extra.

## Other years and other Boxsters

The evidence points to continuity in the main mechanical specification. The launch brochure published in 2008 already gives 188 kW / 255 PS and the choice of manual or PDK, the January 2010 brochure repeats them, and Porsche's 2012 North American technical sheet corroborates the same engine and transmission combination. That is continuity in the mechanicals, not proof that every UK colour, package, price or availability condition stayed the same through 2012.[^years]

The [family introduction](../01-the-2010-boxster-2-9-in-the-987-2-family/index.mdx#the-ordinary-2.9-and-its-relatives) explains how the S, Spyder and Black Edition differ. Each has its own engine, chassis and equipment specification, and the component chapters compare only the differences that matter to each system.[^variants]

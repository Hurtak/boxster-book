# Cabin, seating and climate

The seat fixes the driver's position relative to the pedals, wheel and instruments. The ventilation system manages the air around that position: where it comes from, where it goes, and how much heat and moisture it carries. Both serve comfort, but by different mechanisms.

The 2010 UK Boxster 2.9 has seats with manual fore-and-aft and height adjustment, electric backrests and Alcantara centres. Air conditioning is standard; automatic climate control is optional, with its own controls and display.[^baseline]

## A seat is a support structure

A seat has a load-bearing frame, a sprung cushion, shaped foam and a cover. The cushion sits on springs in the base frame; a separate backrest frame carries the padding and integrated head restraint. The cushion spreads the occupant's weight, the backrest supports the upper body and raised bolsters resist sideways movement. Softness and support are separate qualities: a soft centre can sit between firm bolsters, and reshaping the bolsters changes where the body is held.

The base slides on runners, setting pedal reach. Height changes eye level, headroom and the angle of the legs to the pedals. Backrest angle moves the shoulders and changes reach to the wheel. Setting one to an extreme and compensating with the others rarely gives a relaxed position.

The standard seat and optional ordinary sports seat adjust the same way. A hand lever releases the runner lock, a pumping lever sets the height and an electric switch sets the backrest angle. The head restraint moves with the backrest and has no separate height adjustment. The sports seat adds firmer upholstery and more pronounced side support.[^seat-operation]

Start with pedal reach, then use seat height, backrest angle and the column's height and reach adjustment to bring the controls comfortably to hand. Porsche's handbook describes a slightly bent leg with the manual car's clutch pressed, and shoulders staying against the backrest when setting steering reach. Adjust only when stationary, and check that the runner and column locks have engaged.[^seat-operation]

The gearbox matters here: the manual driver needs full clutch travel, while a PDK car has no clutch pedal. The seat's restraint and airbag functions are covered in [Occupant protection and system integration](../18-occupant-protection-and-system-integration/index.mdx).

## Electric seats, adjustable bolsters and the bucket

The **comfort seat with driver memory** adjusts fore-and-aft position, height, backrest, cushion tilt and lumbar support electrically. Lumbar adjustment changes the backrest's curve rather than reclining it. Memory recalls the driver's seat and exterior-mirror positions, which helps when several people drive the car.[^seat-options]

The **adaptive sports seat** adds electrically adjustable bolsters in the cushion and backrest, changing lateral support without moving the whole seat. The brochure excludes the bolster positions from the driver's memory. The lumbar and bolster supports are pneumatic, unlike the seat's other adjustments: a small electric air pump and valves fill inflatable elements, which shape the backrest for lumbar support and, on the adaptive seat, the sides of the cushion and backrest. They are separate from the seat's restraint airbag.[^seat-options]

The **sports bucket seat** takes another route. A shaped glass- and carbon-fibre-reinforced backrest shell gives deep side support with less reliance on adjustment. It keeps manual fore-and-aft movement and a backrest that folds for access behind the seat, which is not an adjustable recline. The handbook excludes the bucket from its ordinary height and backrest-angle instructions.[^seat-options]

## Heated and ventilated seats

Seat heating puts electrical warmth next to the occupant without waiting for the cabin to warm up; it has two settings. Ventilation works differently. Fans draw air in through the seat's perforations and on through permeable material in the cushion and backrest, then discharge it into the cabin, carrying warm, moist air away from the contact area. Vented foam, air-distribution layers and fan modules in both cushion and backrest form a separate assembly under the cover.[^ventilation]

It depends on air movement, breathable layers and cabin conditions; there is no refrigerant evaporator in the cushion. Heating and ventilation can run together, since warmth and moisture removal do not conflict. Porsche's technical introduction requires at least about 15°C at the seat and interior temperature sensors, and the handbook specifies that the engine is running.[^ventilation]

Blocked perforations or an airtight cover defeat the system. Its three settings vary fan operation, separately from the two heating settings. Leather alone does not prove the fans are fitted; the prerequisites are in the options table below.

## Two heat circuits, one stream of cabin air

The heater draws its warmth from the engine's **coolant circuit**. Hot coolant flows through a small heat exchanger in the front heater unit, and air crossing its fins picks up heat while the coolant continues round its closed circuit. Supply and return run in separate underbody pipes and flexible hoses, sealed where they pass through the body. Air flaps set how much heating the air receives. Only heat, not coolant, reaches the cabin air.[^hardware]

That ties cabin heat to engine warm-up. Just after a cold start the coolant has little heat to spare, and more blower speed moves more air without making the heat exchanger hotter. The engine side is covered in [Lubrication, cooling and exhaust](../07-lubrication-cooling-and-exhaust/index.mdx).

Air conditioning moves heat the other way. Refrigerant evaporates in the cabin unit's **evaporator**, absorbing heat from the passing air. The engine-driven compressor raises its pressure, the front condensers give the heat to outside air, and an expansion valve meters refrigerant back into the low-pressure side. A **receiver-drier** between the condensers and expansion valve stores liquid refrigerant, filters it and absorbs moisture inside the sealed circuit, which is different from drying the cabin air. Because the compressor adds work, the condensers reject both the cabin's heat and the energy that drives the cycle.[^refrigeration]

On this generation the compressor has no magnetic clutch. Its shaft turns whenever the engine runs, and an electronic valve varies its delivery; A/C OFF sets delivery to zero rather than stopping the shaft. A turning belt therefore says nothing about cooling output.[^compressor]

<Figure src={illustration7} alt={"Separate loops show hot engine coolant delivering heat to a heater core and refrigerant taking cabin heat through an evaporator to the front condensers, with a receiver-drier before the expansion valve. Only heat crosses between the fluids and the cabin air."}>

Two sealed loops share one stream of cabin air: coolant adds heat at the heater core, while refrigerant removes it at the evaporator and releases it at the front condensers.

</Figure>

Cooling also dries the air. When the evaporator is below the air's dew point, water condenses on it and drains away, and the heater can then rewarm the dried air. Warm air from the vents and active air conditioning can work together, which is useful for clear glass. Water under the car after air-conditioning use can be normal condensate.[^air-operation]

## Choosing the air route

Fresh air enters near the base of the windscreen and passes a filter that traps airborne particles. The blower pushes it through the unit and ducts, and outlet flaps distribute it between the central and side vents, footwells and windscreen. The dashboard vents can also be aimed and opened individually.[^air-operation]

**Recirculation** shuts off outside air and reuses cabin air. That helps briefly when outside air is unpleasant or a hot cabin needs cooling. But occupants keep adding moisture from breath and wet clothing, and reused air that is not dried enough mists cold glass. Fresh air, heat, air movement and evaporator drying each address a different part of the problem.

<Diagram id="cabin-air-route" poster={illustration1} alt={"With fresh air selected, the blower moves air through an evaporator with A/C enabled, around the heater and towards the face vents. Recirculation changes the source; heater contribution changes heat addition; outlet selection changes the destination."} />

The diagram's demisting preset selects fresh air, windscreen delivery, heat and available A/C drying. It shows the principle, not every interlock of the Porsche panel, and calculates no cabin temperature, humidity or clearing time.

The standard **manually controlled air conditioning** lets the driver set heat, blower speed and distribution, with temperature shown as bars. Optional **automatic climate control** works to a selected cabin temperature using temperature and solar sensors. In AUTO it adjusts air quantity and distribution as conditions change, and the driver can still intervene. The set temperature is a target for the controller, not the temperature the vent air will always have.[^climate]

To demist, the air must reach the glass; a strong but badly aimed face vent cannot replace the windscreen outlets. Porsche tells the driver to end recirculation if the windows mist and use the windscreen demisting function. Compressor output also has temperature and engine-load limits, so "A/C selected" does not mean continuous cooling in every condition.[^air-operation]

## Instruments and everyday controls

The ordinary 2.9 has **black instrument faces**: an analogue rev counter in the centre with a digital speed readout, and an analogue speedometer, trip and total distance on the left dial. The on-board computer shows range, average consumption and, when fitted, tyre pressures; PDK adds selector and gear information. The S's aluminium-coloured faces, pictured below, are not the base specification.[^instruments]

The column stalks put indicators and main beam, wipers and on-board-computer selection near the driver. Optional **cruise control, code 454**, adds a separate stalk that holds a chosen road speed by requesting engine output, over a 30–240 km/h range according to the UK catalogue. Brake or clutch operation cancels it, and it sets speed only; it does not follow the distance to another vehicle.[^cruise]

The standard sports steering wheel carries the steering and its airbag. A multifunction wheel adds audio and, with the relevant equipment, telephone and navigation controls. PDK switches or paddles request gear changes, and the [two arrangements](../09-pdk-transmission/index.mdx#the-selector-and-steering-wheel-controls) differ. A wheel's appearance cannot establish every function behind it.[^instruments]

On the lower console, the roof and any fitted driving-mode switches control their own systems; [the integration matrix](../18-occupant-protection-and-system-integration/index.mdx#options-on-the-2010-2.9) explains the equipment dependencies. Storage includes door and console compartments, a locking glovebox with retractable cupholders above it, and a rear-bulkhead box that shares its space with the optional BOSE installation.[^instruments]

## The S, Spyder and Black Edition

The **S** has the same seat and climate option families in the January 2010 UK table.[^option-codes]

The **Spyder** starts with sports bucket seats and omits air conditioning as part of its lighter specification. Its January 2010 UK brochure lists sports seats P77 and automatic climate control 573 as alternatives or additions, and heated seats 342 require **both sports seats and automatic climate control**.[^spyder]

The **Boxster S Black Edition** instead includes automatic climate control in the UK March 2011 price list, with partial-leather seats offering manual fore-and-aft and height adjustment and electric backrests.[^black]

## Identification and ageing

An AUTO button and numerical temperature setting identify the automatic climate panel; the standard panel shows bars. Seat-side switches, memory buttons and the shell's shape identify the seat type. Perforations or a smart retrim do not prove factory ventilation.

When something fails, ask which has changed: position, airflow, heat or moisture removal. A restricted filter can reduce the air reaching every outlet, while a blocked duct affects the outlets it feeds. A blower fault changes how much air arrives, a flap fault can send it to the wrong place, and a refrigeration or heater fault can deliver plenty of air at the wrong temperature. Seat runners, adjustment drives, foam, perforated trim and fans each wear in their own way.

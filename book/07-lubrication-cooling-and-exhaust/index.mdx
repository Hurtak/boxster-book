# Lubrication, cooling and exhaust

The 2010 Boxster 2.9 gets rid of its heat in three connected ways. Oil keeps the moving surfaces apart and carries heat away from them. Coolant collects heat from the engine and sheds it through radiators at the front. Exhaust gas carries a large share of the combustion heat out through the catalysts and silencers to the central tailpipe. [Engine construction and valve gear](../05-engine-construction-and-valve-gear/index.mdx) describes the machinery that makes the heat; this chapter follows what leaves it.

## Oil collection and pressure supply

Porsche calls the 2.9's system **integrated dry-sump lubrication**. The oil reservoir is inside the engine, below the moving parts, and oil is pumped out of the cylinder heads rather than left to drain back by gravity alone. The January 2010 brochure counts four of these return pumps and a fifth that feeds the lubrication points.[^fluid-integrated]

**Scavenge pumps** pull used oil back to the reservoir; the **pressure pump** draws from that supply and pushes it round the lubrication circuit. Each needs the other: a full reservoir is no use if the pressure pump cannot pick up oil under braking or cornering, and pressure at the pump cannot make up indefinitely for oil held up elsewhere in the engine.

Each head has two extraction points, with a scavenge pump for each. All five pumps sit on one shaft in the oil-pan area, driven from the crankshaft by their own chain, separate from the two camshaft chains. This reaches both of the flat engine's widely separated heads while keeping the reservoir inside it. Porsche also added a sheet-metal panel separating the crankcase from the oil pan.[^fluid-pumps]

<Figure src={illustration2} alt={"Four pumps return oil from the two heads; the fifth supplies the pressure circuit. The drawing groups functions and does not specify the order of every filter, cooler and gallery passage."}>

Four scavenge pumps return oil from the two heads; the fifth feeds the pressure circuit. The drawing groups the pumps by job and does not show the order of every filter, cooler and gallery passage.

</Figure>

The pressurised oil reaches the bearings and the oil-fed mechanisms described in [engine construction and valve gear](../05-engine-construction-and-valve-gear/index.mdx). In a plain bearing, the turning shaft drags oil into the narrowing gap and builds a load-carrying film, which a steady supply renews while taking heat away. Pressure, flow, temperature and bearing condition all matter, and the oil-level reading measures none of them.

The pressure pump varies its delivery to suit demand. The engine management sets a control valve from engine speed, load, oil temperature and the expected change in engine speed. Oil pressure from that valve slides one pump gear along its axis, changing its overlap with the fixed gear; less overlap cuts both delivery and the effort needed to turn the pump.[^fluid-demand] The control is electronic, but the crankshaft still drives the pump and the four scavenge sections keep working regardless. That is how the same system can be described both as five pumps and as a demand-controlled oil pump.

The [crankcase-ventilation separator](../06-air-fuel-ignition-and-engine-control/index.mdx#crankcase-ventilation-and-oil-separation) handles a different flow, recovering oil mist from blow-by gases before they return to the intake.

## Checking the oil level

The level is read through the on-board computer. Porsche specifies a stationary car on level ground, the engine idling and the oil at operating temperature, at least **60 °C**. Select the oil menu and wait for the measurement to finish. A slope or a different engine state moves the oil and spoils the comparison.[^fluid-level]

The segmented display shows the permitted band, not the total oil in the engine, and its top mark is a limit, not a target. A good level also proves nothing about the pressure circuit, which has its own warning.

A later Porsche bulletin covering model years 2009–2012 explains that an oil-level warning can stay on after topping up until the prescribed confirmation measurement is done. Porsche issued it because level sensors that worked were being replaced unnecessarily. Complete the measurement before blaming the sensor.[^fluid-confirm]

## Carrying heat to the front

Long coolant pipes and flexible connections link the engine to radiators at the front, where they meet fresh air. The two main radiator modules sit behind the left and right front openings, each with its own core, rear fan and shroud, front air guides, and supply and return connections in Porsche's parts drawings. Separate distributor tubes feed the two radiators side by side, not one after the other. The centre radiator has its own parts plate and configuration qualifiers.[^fluid-pet-cooling]

<Figure src={illustration3} alt={"Porsche's period cutaway places the radiator installation at the front and the engine, rear silencers and coolant expansion tank towards the rear. This is the brochure's Boxster S illustration, used to show the shared mid-engine packaging rather than to identify the reference car's options. © Porsche AG."}>

Boxster S cutaway, showing the layout the 2.9 shares: radiators at the front; engine, rear silencers and coolant expansion tank at the back. Factory illustration: Porsche AG, *The Boxster* (UK, 01/10), pp. 40–41. [Source](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=18).

</Figure>

The pump circulates coolant through the engine's internal jackets, where it picks up heat, then through the radiators' tubes and fins, where air carries the heat away. Both flows are needed: a clear coolant circuit cannot make up for blocked airflow.

Inside the engine, cross-flow cooling spreads coolant across the cylinder heads. The new generation gained revised passages, including extra cooling around the exhaust-valve seats, where gas leaving the combustion chamber concentrates the heat. The pump is a separate belt-driven module on the outside of the crankcase, on the bank 1–3 side.[^fluid-engine-cooling]

The **thermostat** controls how much coolant reaches the radiators. From cold it restricts the long radiator circuit, so coolant takes a shorter path that brings the engine up to working temperature. As it warms, the thermostat opens the radiator path progressively, through intermediate positions, not just the shut and open states of teaching diagrams. The general principle is a temperature-sensitive element that expands and pushes a valve open against a spring.[^fluid-thermostat]

<Diagram id="fluid-cooling-path" poster={illustration1} alt={"During warm-up, coolant keeps circulating through the engine's short return path while the thermostat restricts the front radiator route. Once warm, the radiator circuit opens; electric fans can assist airflow when required."} />

Its opening temperature does not fix the temperature of every part of the engine; heat output, radiator capacity, air temperature and airflow still set the balance.

The electric radiator fans draw air through the front modules, which matters most at low road speed. The engine-compartment blower is a different fan that ventilates the enclosed engine space; Porsche's handbook notes that it may keep running, or start again, after switch-off while the compartment temperature is monitored.[^fluid-fans]

The **expansion tank** gives the coolant room to expand as it warms, and the cap holds the system under pressure. The filler sits under the service flap in the rear luggage compartment, in a service cup that carries separate oil and coolant caps; the parts drawing lists the moulded tank, bleed valve, level switch and vent lines separately from the cup and its hose. Check the marked level with the engine cold and the car level. The cap is easy to reach, but opened hot it can release pressurised coolant.[^fluid-tank]

After work on so long a circuit, trapped air can displace coolant and block heat transfer. Correct filling and bleeding are part of the repair, not something to judge from one glance at the temperature gauge.

## Where oil and coolant exchange heat

The engine's oil-to-coolant heat exchanger runs the two liquids through separate passages divided by metal walls: heat crosses, while the liquids stay separate. Porsche's engine-lubrication catalogue places the exchanger on the oil-conducting housing. A removable cup beneath the neighbouring part of that housing holds the replaceable filter insert, and a separate oil-pressure sender mounts there too. The oil-level sender belongs to the sump. Filtering the oil, measuring its pressure and measuring its level are three separate jobs.[^fluid-exchanger]

<Figure src={illustration4} alt={"The exchanger transfers heat across a metal wall while keeping oil and coolant separate. The arrows depict hotter oil losing heat to cooler coolant; the direction reverses if their relative temperatures reverse."}>

Heat crosses the wall from the hotter fluid to the cooler one, while oil and coolant stay apart. The arrows show hot oil warming the coolant; when the coolant is warmer, heat flows the other way.

</Figure>

"Oil cooler" describes an important part of its job, but heat always flows from hotter to cooler. When the coolant is warmer, it helps warm the oil; when the oil is warmer, its heat passes to the coolant and on to the radiators. The two never have to reach the same temperature. Oil and coolant heat different masses, follow different paths and pick up heat in different places, so a steady coolant gauge does not mean all the oil is equally warm.

## The exhaust: cleaning and silencing

Each cylinder bank pushes its exhaust pulses into its own manifold. The system must let the gas out freely, convert pollutants, contain noise and fit hot parts around the rear power unit. Porsche's technical introduction describes two treatment paths, one per bank, joined by connecting pipes at the silencers before the gas leaves through the centre.[^fluid-exhaust-route]

Each bank has one main catalytic converter built into its manifold, containing **two ceramic monoliths**, or bricks. The gas passes an upstream oxygen sensor, the first brick, a downstream oxygen sensor and the second brick before reaching the main silencer. That makes four oxygen sensors and four bricks, but only two converters.[^fluid-exhaust-route]

<Figure src={illustration5} alt={"Each bank has two catalyst bricks within one manifold/converter assembly, with an oxygen sensor before the first brick and another between the bricks. The schematic shows gas-treatment sequence and connected silencers."}>

Each bank's manifold and converter assembly holds two catalyst bricks, with one oxygen sensor before the first brick and another between them. Connecting pipes join the two silencers.

</Figure>

Those sensors feed the [mixture-feedback system](../06-air-fuel-ignition-and-engine-control/index.mdx#mixture-feedback-checking-the-result) explained in the previous chapter.

The catalyst provides active surfaces on which reactions convert pollutants, and must be hot enough to work. The silencer handles pressure waves and sound: its chambers and passages change how the pulses travel and interact as the gas flows on. The connecting pipes link the banks acoustically as well as combining their flow. Flanged joints connect each manifold and converter to its silencer, heat shields protect nearby parts, and a rear retainer and straps carry the silencers; the catalogue lists manual and PDK versions of the retainer. Little of this can be judged from the tailpipe.

<Figure src={illustration6} alt={"The standard Boxster S exhaust pictured by Porsche shows two manifold/catalyst assemblies, two rear silencers, connecting pipes and twin central outlets. Porsche describes the 2.9 system as matching the S apart from its tailpipe. © Porsche AG."}>

Boxster S exhaust, with twin outlets; the standard 2.9 has a single shaped outlet. Factory illustration: Porsche AG, *The Boxster* (UK, 01/10), p. 39. [Source](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf#page=17).

</Figure>

The standard 2.9 ends in a single shaped stainless-steel tailpipe where the S has two. Porsche's technical account says the 2.9's exhaust otherwise matches the S system. The 2.9 also has the [secondary-air warm-up system](../06-air-fuel-ignition-and-engine-control/index.mdx#cold-running-and-emissions) described under engine control.[^fluid-base-exhaust]


## Options on the 2010 2.9

The UK catalogue offers a **sports exhaust system** for a more pronounced sound. The handbook shows its button, which lights up when the system is selected. Porsche's later parts catalogue gives sports tailpipes and the sports exhaust separate codes, **XLS** and **XLF**: the look and the switchable silencing are separate equipment.[^fluid-exhaust-option]

The factory sports system uses flaps associated with the silencers. Porsche's parts documentation lists a change-over valve, vacuum lines and a vacuum unit. An electrical command switches the vacuum path; the pressure difference moves the actuator, and its linkage positions the flap, giving the gas a different acoustic route. The button therefore depends on electrics, small-bore hoses and moving parts.[^fluid-valves]

Optional PDK adds a second oil-to-coolant exchanger, for the gearbox's clutch and hydraulic oil, separate from the engine's. The gearbox's heat adds to the cooling load; [PDK](../09-pdk-transmission/index.mdx) explains its oil circuits. Check centre-radiator fitment on the car itself, not from the model badge.[^fluid-pdk]

## Changes in other 2.9 model years

The five-pump lubrication, demand control and revised cooling arrived with the 2009 engine generation. The launch brochure advertised the sports exhaust from **April 2009 at the earliest**.[^fluid-years] The [annual matrix](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#annual-equipment-matrix) holds the dated evidence for other years.

## The S, Spyder and Black Edition

The S shares the five-pump lubrication, the cooling principles and the basic exhaust layout. Its standard exhaust differs at the tail, with twin outlets instead of the 2.9's single one.[^fluid-pumps][^fluid-base-exhaust]

The Spyder and Black Edition both list the sports exhaust as an option. Their higher rated output comes from a different engine specification; the sources show no different pump count, reservoir or thermostat calibration for either.[^fluid-special]

## Identification and ageing

To check the cooling, follow the whole chain from the front air openings and radiator faces through the pipes, connections, pump and tank. To identify a sports exhaust, look for its vacuum hoses and flap linkages as well as the silencers. Replacement tailpipe trim alone does not show that the switchable system is fitted.

Leaks, clogged fins, perished connections and seized mechanisms each affect a different link. Start from the circuit the symptom belongs to: an oil-level message, an oil-pressure warning, coolant loss and an unusual exhaust note point to different places.

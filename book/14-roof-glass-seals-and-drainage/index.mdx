# Roof, glass, seals and drainage

The **2010 Boxster 2.9** has an electrically operated fabric roof with a folding frame, a heated glass rear window and a front latch released by hand. Open, it packs into its own compartment behind the seats, leaving both luggage compartments free. Closed, its fabric, lining, glass and seals form a weather enclosure.[^catalogue]

Three systems share the work. The mechanism moves and supports the roof, the glass and seals close the gaps around it, and channels, drip pans and drains let water out of the body. That is why a roof can move perfectly yet leak, or stay dry while a window or linkage develops a fault.

## How the ordinary roof moves

### One motor drives both sides

A **single motor** behind the seats drives the roof. Two flexible shafts carry its rotation to separate left and right gearboxes, where levers and jointed rods turn it into movement of the roof and its compartment cover. Porsche's parts catalogue lists the motor once, the flexible shafts twice and the gearboxes as separate left and right items.[^drive]

These shafts, often called roof cables, transmit **rotation**. They are not the tension cables that shape the fabric, nor the regulator cables that move the door glass.

The gearboxes reduce speed to drive the levers. Rods with articulated ends keep pushing as their angles change, and the frame's pivots hold the sections to a repeatable folding path, letting a large roof fit a short compartment.

<Figure src={illustration4} alt={"Connection map showing one roof motor driving left and right gearboxes through two flexible shafts, then levers and rods moving the roof frame and compartment cover."}>

How the 2.9's single motor reaches both sides of the roof. In this original schematic, the branches show what drives what, not where the parts sit or how the shafts are routed.

</Figure>

The two sides must stay in step: if one advances farther than the other, the frame twists as well as folds. The [ageing table](#reading-the-signs-of-age) links uneven movement and motor noise to the parts worth checking.

### The cover makes room for the folding frame

The painted panel directly behind the roof is the **convertible-top compartment cover**, separate from the rear luggage lid. As the roof opens, the cover rises so the frame can travel rearwards and fold, then closes over it. The fabric and lining fold with the frame; the glass rear window has to be stowed as a rigid panel.[^drive]

The cloth is only the outer layer. Bows and the frame give the roof its shape, tension wires guide the cloth's edges, and a moulded headliner lines the inside; Porsche lists the covering, tension wires, headliner and rear-window heating loom separately. A crease, a misplaced edge and a bent frame member are therefore different faults. Cloth can also wear where a bad fold keeps rubbing it, even though the drive still completes its travel.

### Latch and switches

At the windscreen header, a lever and hook release or secure the roof. Powered closing brings the roof forward, but the driver still fastens the front latch by hand. Left and right centring journals locate the front edge and the catch holds it against the header seal. The roof is powered; its front locking is not automatic.[^operation]

The console switch requests movement; small switches elsewhere report the mechanism's position. Porsche's 987 workshop information places a roof microswitch at the front lock, separate from the driver's switch. It reports a condition but supplies no force, which matters when the roof looks right but the system does not recognise its position.[^switch] Likewise, the handbook's opening or closing message clears only at the end position, so a roof that looks nearly done may still be short of it.[^operation]

Porsche's UK catalogue quotes **12 seconds** to open and an operating limit of **50 km/h, approximately 30 mph**. The MY2010 North American handbook adds that the car needs clearance and even support, forbids operation below **0°C** and warns against obstructions. The car's own market handbook gives the full conditions and sequence.[^operation]

## Glass and weather sealing

### Why the door glass drops slightly

The door glass has no frame. When it is fully raised with the door shut, its top edge meets the roof's seals and cannot slide sideways out of that contact as the door opens. So it first drops a little.

The handbook specifies a drop of a few millimetres when the door opens and a rise when it closes, coordinated by door-handle sensing and the window mechanism. Inside each door, an electric motor drives cables around a two-rail regulator. The parts catalogue lists the regulator, motor and a window-regulator microswitch among the door-handle components.[^windows]

<Figure src={illustration6} alt={"Conceptual end sections show raised frameless glass meeting a resilient roof seal, then the glass lowered slightly to give clearance before the door swings outwards."}>

The raised glass meets the roof seal; dropping it slightly frees it before the door swings out. In this original schematic, the drop and seal section are exaggerated and are not adjustment figures.

</Figure>

The windows also lower during roof movement, so the seals are not dragged over fully raised glass. That and the door drop meet related clearance needs but are separate events, and neither shows that the roof drive is healthy.

The window remembers its end position. After the battery has been disconnected and reconnected, the glass will not rise automatically when the door closes until that position has been stored again.[^windows]

### Seals need the right contact

The roof has separate seals along its front frame, sides and rear. Each works by pressing resilient material against a correctly placed neighbouring surface. Enough compression seals; too much force, a damaged lip or a misplaced glass edge distorts the seal instead. Porsche's catalogue gives the roof-frame, B-pillar and roof-arch seals their own retaining strips, separate from the door-opening seals and the inner and outer strips along the top of the door, beside the glass.[^seals]

Wind noise can come from misalignment as well as aged rubber. A visible gap shows where to look, not which part moved first, and a thicker seal fitted to cure every leak can hide the real relationship between frame, glass and original sealing surfaces.

The rear window is **heated glass**, warmed by resistance elements to clear condensation. Heating helps visibility; the window's mounting and seals keep water out. A misted rear window can simply mean humid cabin air meeting cold glass and is not, on its own, proof of a leak. The handbook also notes that the optional wind deflector's centre section can restrict ventilation of this area with the roof closed.[^glass]

## Water needs a way out

A waterproof cloth is not enough. Water running off the fabric must pass edges, moving joints and the compartment around the folded roof. The ordinary Boxster catches it there in a drip pan on each side, each with a filter element and drain pipe. Porsche also lists water-drain plates beside the drive mechanism and two drainage hoses at the compartment seal. These are different collection parts, not several names for one hose, and the compartment is not simply an empty space behind the seats.[^drains]

Seals and drains work in sequence: a seal limits what crosses a joint, and a drain removes what collects on the wet side. Water can reach a collection pan by design while the cabin stays dry.

The left water-collecting tray, 987 561 805 01. Its shallow, contoured shape wraps around neighbouring roof and body parts; the drainage diagram below flattens it out to show the water flow. Porsche's Technical Information **29/09, ENU 5393** describes one way this fails. Leaves and debris can gather between the roof and its compartment lid and drop into the compartment when the roof opens. If they block the drains, water can reach the interior, even with an intact cloth and sound upper seals.[^drain-bulletin]

<Figure src={visualinstalleddrainfactoryjpg} alt="Porsche’s installed-location drawing shows the side-pan opening by the roof mechanism, then the mesh filter placed over it.">

Installed drain location in Porsche’s workshop illustration: opening above, mesh filter (1) below, beside the mechanism and compartment trim. This is a **factory drawing, not a photograph**. Source: Porsche TI 29/09 ENU 5393, Figure 1; the saved reference retains its provenance. The illustration locates the opening without inventing the downstream pipe route.

</Figure>

<Diagram id="roof-drainage" poster={illustration2} alt={"With an intact upper seal, a clear drip-pan outlet lets water leave the body; blocking the same outlet lets water accumulate and eventually overflow."} />

The diagram changes only the outlet and the amount of water added. Clear, the outlet lets water escape; blocked, the same pan becomes a reservoir. The level shown is illustrative, not a Boxster capacity.

Trace a leak upstream: the wettest carpet shows where water settles, not necessarily where it got in. A blocked drain, a damaged collection part and a failed seal need different repairs, and one dirty drain does not prove the other water paths are sound.

The drain filters catch debris, which then builds up on the filters. Porsche still requires the compartment to be inspected and cleaned regularly.[^drain-bulletin]

Options on the 2010 2.9

The January 2010 UK catalogue shows five fabric colours: **Black, Stone Grey, Metropole Blue, Cocoa and Red**. The separate price list gives the recommended paint and interior combinations.[^options]

The optional **three-piece wind deflector, code 551**, has outer pieces that fit in the roll-over bars and a removable centre section between them. With the roof open it reduces turbulence around the occupants' heads and shoulders. The catalogue describes it in combination with the rear-bulkhead storage box or the BOSE® Surround Sound System. It neither keeps out rain nor forms part of the roof structure.[^options]

A Tequipment sheet dated **28 January 2010** also lists the complete hardtop as an accessory for the Boxster 987 from 2005.[^tequipment] Factory-fitted or added later, it needs the correct mounting equipment: the handbook says cars delivered without a hardtop lack the inserts for its rear pivot locks and need those parts before it is first fitted. A used hardtop's shape and paint match do not show that a car is ready for it. Check the hardtop, mounting parts and electrical connection against the car; "fits a 987" is not a complete installation specification.[^hardtop]

<Heading level={2} id="s-black-edition-and-spyder">

The S, Spyder and Black Edition

</Heading>

The **Boxster S** shares the powered folding-roof design and the UK catalogue's hardtop and wind-deflector options; check the parts information for individual components and trim versions.[^drive]

### The Spyder takes a different approach

The **987 Boxster Spyder** replaces the electric folding roof with a two-piece **sunshield and weather protector** that the driver fits and removes by hand. The sunshield covers the cabin and the weather protector closes the opening behind it. There are no roof motors, the pieces stow in the front of the rear luggage compartment, and Porsche quoted a 21 kg saving.[^spyder]

<Figure src={illustration9} alt={"The 987 Boxster Spyder with its lightweight sunshield in place and the rear opening exposed, showing why it is distinct from the ordinary folding roof."}>

Spyder with its sunshield fitted; a separate weather protector closes the rear opening. Photo: Porsche AG, *The Boxster Spyder* (UK, 01/10), pp. 20–21. [Source](https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/brochures2/93/2010%20987%20Boxster%20Spyder.pdf#page=9).

</Figure>

**With the Spyder's hood closed, Porsche specifies a maximum of 200 km/h (124 mph), for both manual and PDK cars.** This closed-hood restriction is distinct from the ordinary roof's 50 km/h operating limit, and the Spyder's higher hood-open maximum speeds are separate figures.[^spyder]

### The Black Edition keeps the powered roof

The **Boxster S Black Edition** keeps the electrically operated fabric roof, in black with a Black Edition marking, and has the wind deflector as standard.[^black]

<Heading level={2} id="reading-the-signs-of-age">

Identification and ageing

</Heading>

Sort the symptom before blaming a part:

| Observation | What it helps distinguish |
| --- | --- |
| No movement, with no motor sound | An electrical request, supply or recognised-condition issue remains possible; the symptom alone does not condemn the motor. |
| Motor sound with little or uneven movement | The mechanical drive path and coordination need consideration. |
| One cloth edge repeatedly folds incorrectly | Fabric guidance and tension deserve attention as well as the main frame. |
| Glass misses its short drop | Window control and door sensing are separate from roof folding. |
| Water remains in a collection area | Drainage needs investigation before assuming an upper seal has failed. |
| Wind noise with no water ingress | Glass or roof contact may differ even where rain is still excluded. |

The fabric also ages from sunlight, abrasion and dirt. Porsche's care instructions distinguish suitable roof cleaning from indiscriminate high-pressure washing or hot wax, and say to fold the roof only when clean and dry. Damp fabric left folded in long storage can mark even without a mechanical fault.[^care]

If the roof moves unevenly, stop and find out why. Forcing the frame adds load, and workshop access and adjustment require the specified supported positions and procedures.

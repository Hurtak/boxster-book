## Tyre principles

- Continental, [Tyre components](https://www.continental-tires.com/au/en/tyre-knowledge/tire-components/), opened and read: tread/belt versus casing, radial ply, inner liner, sidewall and bead functions. Generic tyre mechanism, not a claim about the exact tyre supplier fitted in 2010.
- Michelin, [What can cause excessive or uneven tyre wear?](https://www.michelin.co.uk/auto/advice/change-tyres/tyre-wear), updated 22 July 2025, opened and read: underinflation, flexing/heat, pressure/wear, cold checks. No pressure value imported from a generic tyre site.
- Contact-patch discussion is qualitative. The chapter explicitly excludes an exact area = load/pressure prediction; carcass forces and nonuniform contact pressure matter.

## Original diagrams and numerical contract

1. `steering-hydraulic.svg`: conceptual driver/shaft/pinion/rack/track-rod path plus pump/reservoir assistance circuit. Not a hose-routing map, measured rack or replacement-parts assembly drawing.
2. `wheel-offset.svg`: centre plane, bead-seat width and positive mounting-face offset. The equal-width ET55-to-ET45 comparison moves the rim out by 10 mm; illustrative alteration only.
3. `diagrams/tyres-rolling-size/`: useful native-button/slider D3 comparison, with `poster.svg`. Initial state is rear tyres, comparison 18-inch, road speed 60 mph. Rims/tyres are drawn to a common geometric scale. Input labels omit Z solely to shorten illustration text; manuscript retains Porsche's ZR notation.

Diameter = rim inches × 25.4 + 2 × width mm × aspect ÷ 100. Circumference = π × diameter. Wheel rpm = mph × 1609.344 ÷ 60 ÷ circumference metres. These are nominal geometric estimates, explicitly not loaded circumference, a speedometer calibration or an approval tool.

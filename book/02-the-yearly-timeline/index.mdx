# The yearly timeline

Most of what defines the 2010 Boxster 2.9 arrived with the 2009 facelift. The Spyder and the Black Edition then joined the range as very different kinds of Boxster, and in 2012 the 981 Boxster replaced them. This chapter follows those events by calendar year; the reference car's equipment is set out in the [2010 specification and options chapter](../03-the-2010-boxster-2-9-specification-and-options/index.mdx). The questions here are when that car's design arrived, what joined the range afterwards, and which dates actually prove a change.

## Which date answers which question

An announcement is the day Porsche made a product public, and a market introduction is when it expected customers to be able to buy it. Neither tells you when a particular car was built. **Model year** is the manufacturer's specification label, not a promise that the car was built, sold and registered within that calendar year. **Build date** is when the car was made, and **first registration** is when it entered the registration system. A car held in stock can cross a calendar boundary between those events, and an import can gain a new registration record without becoming a later technical version.

Brochures carry one more date: their own publication. The UK launch catalogue is marked **08/08** and this book's reference catalogue **01/10**. Those marks date the documents, not the cars. They do not make the facelift a 2008 model-year car, and they do not reveal when an option first reached production. Later service-parts catalogues have their own edition dates and applicability ranges, which help identify parts but cannot show when a model was announced or first sold.[^timeline-catalogues]

## Late 2008: the 2.9 is announced

Porsche announced the revised Boxster and Cayman on **19 November 2008**. The ordinary Boxster gained a new 2.9-litre engine producing 188 kW / 255 PS, and the S rose to 228 kW / 310 PS. Both now had a six-speed manual as standard, and PDK replaced Tiptronic S as the automatic option.[^timeline-launch]

The outgoing ordinary Boxster was a 2.7: 2,687 cm³, 180 kW and a five-speed manual, with a five-speed Tiptronic S as the automatic option. That is the 2010 2.9's real predecessor, even when an example was registered late or advertised simply as a “2009 Boxster”.[^timeline-predecessor]

The new cars became available in 2009. A contemporary Porsche SE results release confirms that the new Boxster and Cayman had been on sale **since February 2009**, which is firmer evidence of arrival than a prelaunch catalogue date.[^timeline-arrival]

## 2009: the new range reaches customers

From February, buyers could have the new-generation flat-six with the six-speed manual or the optional seven-speed PDK. The 2.9 uses port injection and the S direct fuel injection, or DFI, as [the family overview](../01-the-2010-boxster-2-9-in-the-987-2-family/index.mdx#the-ordinary-2.9-and-its-relatives) explains.[^timeline-injection]

The front and rear ends, headlamps and LED rear lamps were all new. Porsche also retuned the chassis and steering, introduced new wheels, and gave PSM two new functions: brake precharging and brake assistance.[^timeline-launch]

Not every option was ready for the first deliveries. The early UK catalogue gave two items planned earliest dates:[^timeline-launch-options]

| Launch-catalogue choice | What the catalogue establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Sports exhaust, XLF | Available from April 2009 **at the earliest** |
| Mechanical locking rear differential, 220 | Available from May 2009 **at the earliest**, with manual transmission and 18- or 19-inch wheels |

Neither date tells us when the first customer cars with these options were delivered, which matters for cars built close to launch.

On **31 August 2009** Porsche began fitting extra filters to the drains in the rear roof compartment, with a retrofit for earlier cars. This is a build-date boundary, explained in [roof drainage](../14-roof-glass-seals-and-drainage/index.mdx#water-needs-a-way-out).[^timeline-drains]

## 2010: the reference car and the Spyder

The January 2010 UK brochure shows the ordinary 2.9's headline engine and transmission specification unchanged: 2,893 cm³, 188 kW / 255 PS and 290 Nm, with a manual or PDK. The S keeps its 228 kW / 310 PS.[^timeline-reference]

The same catalogue lists the sports steering wheel with separate PDK paddles, option 840, alongside the wheel with switches. That proves the paddle wheel was in this edition, not when the first UK car was ordered or built with one. Porsche's later parts drawing treats the wheel and steering column as separate assemblies, so a paddle wheel fitted today is a clue to the equipment, not proof of the build date or original specification.[^timeline-paddles]

The year's big addition was the **Boxster Spyder**. Porsche's Dutch release of **6 November 2009** planned its market introduction for **February 2010**, a planned date rather than a record of first delivery. Porsche presented it as a third production model beside the Boxster and S, distinct from earlier limited-series Boxsters that had used the Spyder name.[^timeline-spyder-arrival]

The Spyder was built around lightness. Its 235 kW / 320 PS 3.4-litre DFI engine came with a lighter body and cabin, lower sports suspension and a standard mechanical locking differential. In place of the electrically folding roof, it has a lightweight hood made of a separate sunshield and weather protector, both fitted by hand. Its January 2010 UK brochure lists radio and air conditioning as additions to a stripped basic specification. This was a separate model's specification, not a change applied to every 2.9.[^timeline-spyder-brochure]

Porsche Classic's later model summary heads the Spyder **2011**, but the contemporary Dutch announcement planned its introduction for February 2010. Neither that announcement nor the January 2010 brochure makes the Spyder a European MY2009 or MY2010 car.[^timeline-classic]

The reference brochure also planned a change of paint palette during 2010. The reference brochure advertised **Platinum Silver Metallic, Ice Blue Metallic and Amethyst Metallic** for **September 2010 at the earliest**, and listed **Arctic Silver Metallic and Atlas Grey Metallic** only through **July 2010**. It prints “Artic Silver”; the atlas uses the familiar spelling. These are catalogue conditions, not confirmed production dates or a complete colour history.[^timeline-colours] The [annual equipment matrix](../19-reference-atlas-and-index/index.mdx#annual-equipment-matrix) collects them with the mechanical carryovers and the later UK entries that remain unresolved.

## 2011: the Black Edition

The sources reviewed do not reconstruct a complete 2011 UK specification for the ordinary 2.9, and its annual colour, price, software and option changes are thinly documented.

The year's dated event is the **Boxster S Black Edition**. Porsche's Dutch announcement of **2 February 2011** planned availability there from **March 2011**. Its engine produced 235 kW / 320 PS, against the ordinary S's 228 kW / 310 PS, and the edition was limited to **987 cars worldwide**.[^timeline-black-arrival]

The Black Edition paired its black appearance with extra equipment. In the UK, the March price list included PCM/navigation, Sound Package Plus, bi-xenon lights, automatic climate control and cruise control. PDK, PASM, the locking differential, PCCB, Sport Chrono Plus and the sports exhaust remained options, and the car kept the electrically operated hood.[^timeline-black-uk]

This car shows why model-year labels need care. Porsche Cars North America's February 2011 release calls it the **2012 Boxster S Black Edition**. The UK price list says only that its prices apply to vehicles produced in the model year beginning **1 March 2011**. The US label does not mean Europe waited until calendar 2012.[^timeline-black-us]

## 2012: the last 987s and the new 981

The last 987.2 2.9s belong to the same broad mechanical family as the reference car. Porsche's US technical sheet for 2012 still lists the 2.9 with multipoint injection, a six-speed manual and optional PDK, alongside the S, Black Edition and Spyder. It does not settle every UK final-year equipment detail or the date of the last build.[^timeline-final]

The replacement is a different car. Porsche announced it in **January 2012**, and the Dutch release of **12 January** planned the dealer introduction there for **14 April 2012**. The 981 has a new chassis, a longer wheelbase, electromechanical steering and a 2.7-litre direct-injection base engine rated at 195 kW / 265 PS. None of these are final-year improvements to the 987.2 2.9.[^timeline-981]

For a car first registered in 2012, establish the generation, model-year designation, build date and original specification separately, then find the matching brochure. A document headed “2012 Boxster” may describe the outgoing 987 in one market, while an article from the same year describes the 981. Even “2.7” does not settle it, because the earlier 987.1 base car had that nominal capacity too.

## Reading an individual car's history

The timeline places a car in its generation and points to the dated documents that apply to it. From there, compare its build record, present hardware and invoices using [the record-reading method in chapter 3](../03-the-2010-boxster-2-9-specification-and-options/index.mdx#reading-an-individual-cars-record). A registration, a retrofit or a later replacement part belongs to that car's own history.

# Editorial review

The book follows one reference car: the 2010 European/UK Boxster 2.9. The six-speed manual is the standard configuration and PDK is an explicit option, rather than an assumed owner's specification.

## Reading order

The existing numbered order is retained. Chapters 1–3 establish identity, chronology and the reference specification. Chapter 4 locates the assemblies in the body. Chapters 5–7 explain the engine and its supporting systems; chapters 8–10 follow torque to the rear wheels. Chapters 11–13 cover wheel control, steering, tyres and braking. Chapters 14–17 move through the roof, cabin, electrical equipment and entertainment systems. Chapter 18 connects occupant protection with the systems already introduced.

This puts a mechanism before the systems which depend on it: engine construction before engine control, springs and geometry before tyre behaviour, and individual braking functions before the closing integration chapter. No directories are renumbered.

## Where explanations belong

- Chapter 1 owns naming, the European/UK baseline, units and shared reading conventions.
- Chapter 2 owns the range's announcement and introduction chronology. Later chapters record only system-specific changes.
- Chapter 3 owns the reference specification and broad option map. Component chapters explain what those choices do.
- Chapter 5 owns engine construction and valve gear; chapter 6 owns fuel, ignition and control; chapter 7 owns oil, coolant and exhaust flow.
- Chapter 8 owns manual clutch operation; chapter 9 explains the two-clutch PDK arrangement and its controls. Chapter 10 owns differential action and drive joints.
- Chapter 11 owns spring/damper distinctions, geometry and unsprung mass. Chapter 12 owns steering assistance, wheel fitment and tyre behaviour.
- Chapter 13 owns ABS, PSM and hydraulic braking. Chapter 18 refers back to those mechanisms when explaining system integration.
- Chapter 14 owns roof movement, glass clearance and water drainage. Chapter 15 owns seat comfort, heating, ventilation and air conditioning.
- Chapter 16 owns electrical supply, lighting and access/security. Chapter 17 owns sound, navigation and phone connectivity.

Cross-references link to the explanation that owns a concept. A short reminder is useful where it supports the current mechanism; repeating a whole tutorial is not.

## Manuscript conventions

Use clear British English and introduce a technical term at its first useful appearance. Explain the 2010 2.9 mechanism first. Where applicable, close with options, changes in other 2.9 model years, model comparisons, and identification/ageing. Keep dates and quantities attached to the source which establishes them. A missing annual record remains an evidence gap, rather than an invented change or a claim that nothing changed.

Retain local qualifications which affect interpretation: new-disc dimensions are not wear limits; an illustrative rate is not a factory calibration; a comparison photograph depicts its named variant. Shared methodological cautions belong in chapter 1 rather than recurring at every section ending.

Porsche and other primary publications are cited in chapter-local endnotes. Chapter-local research files retain detailed evidence and uncertainty; image credits retain source and processing details. Original diagrams have static fallbacks and visible explanations of any illustrative quantities. Use Sport and Sport Plus consistently in narrative text. Photographs have one concise visible caption with the pictured variant, attribution and source; a separate `alt` attribute preserves the descriptive text for screen readers.

## Reader integration

Cross-chapter Markdown links are converted to internal HTML fragments in both editions, with unknown chapters and headings rejected at build time. Numbered chapter folders remain the source of reading order. The reader preserves the owning chapter when following section and source links, and provides the complete manuscript and static figures without JavaScript.

## Final read-through

The complete 18-chapter manuscript was reviewed in reading order. The existing sequence remains: identity and chronology, reference equipment, body and powertrain, chassis, roof and cabin, electrical and communication systems, then protection and system integration. Chapter 1 now gives the reader that route explicitly.

The line edit reduced the reading text from about 39,700 to 35,300 words, excluding source notes. All chapter titles, section headings, 59 SVG diagrams, 24 interactive figures and 36 sourced images remain. The only numeric forms removed entirely from the narrative were a redundant illustrative 2,400 rpm example and two bibliographic identifiers; their underlying technical explanations and source records remain.

Repeated explanations now point to the chapter which owns them: chapter 2 for chronology, chapter 3 for records and option dependencies, chapter 6 for engine response, chapter 8 for gearing and clutch principles, chapter 9 for PDK functions, chapter 11 for PASM, and chapter 17 for PCM features. Component identification sections retain their specific clues, such as spring markings, fluid circuits, tyre castings and airbag locations. Local operating limits and unresolved evidence are retained.

All 36 photo/factory-image captions were consolidated into single captions. Descriptive alternative text is retained separately, with the same meaning and only normal typographic quote differences. Comparison variants, optional equipment, attribution and source locators remain explicit. Interactive diagrams retain their own model assumptions and print fallbacks.

The portable, web and Pages builds pass, and all 24 automated tests pass after the edit. Validation includes internal fragments, image loading, offline fonts, keyboard controls, source navigation, history, resource cleanup and printing. Caption layouts were inspected at 390, 768 and 1440 pixels; all 36 descriptive alternatives and single credited captions passed the DOM checks. Expanded drafts and reading editions remain local; nothing is staged or published by this pass.

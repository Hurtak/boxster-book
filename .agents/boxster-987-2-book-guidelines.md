# Porsche Boxster 2.9 2010 book guidelines

An outline for an illustrated enthusiast reference centred on the **2010 Porsche Boxster 987.2 2.9-litre**, using Europe and the UK as the main reference markets. This car is the baseline for every chapter, explanation, illustration and specification table. Readers should first understand its components and how they work, then learn which equipment was optional, what changed in other years, and how other models differ.

Use two main parts: **the 2.9-litre Boxster through the years**, followed by **the 2010 2.9-litre Boxster component by component**. Give both substantial space; roughly one third history and two thirds technical explanation is a useful starting point. Keep detailed specification tables and option-code indexes in appendices. Cover the S, Spyder and Black Edition in comparison sections within the relevant chapters.

This is a writing brief with a researched historical foundation. The component bullets define the coverage; they are not a claim that every listed option was available on every variant in every year. Exact annual UK prices, colour changes and running production revisions need dated local documentation before publication.

## Scope and conventions

- **Fixed reference car:** use the model-year 2010 Boxster 2.9 in its standard market specification. Present its factory options as alternatives to that starting point. A particular owner's gearbox, options, build date or registration date has not been specified; keep manual and PDK configurations explicit wherever relevant.
- **Chapter order:** explain the 2010 2.9 first, its 2010 options second, changes to the 2.9 in other years third, and shared applications or differences on the S, Spyder and Black Edition last. If a component has no documented change, say so within the limits of the evidence.
- Cover the facelifted 987, normally called **987.2**, **987 II** or **Gen 2**, principally the 2009–2012 model-year range. Introduce the earlier 987.1 only where it explains a change. Porsche groups the ordinary facelift Boxster and S under 2009–2012 in its model archive. [Porsche Classic Boxster](https://www.porsche.com/uk/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/987b/987b/), [Porsche Classic Boxster S](https://www.porsche.com/uk/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/987b/987b-s/).
- Keep the Boxster 2.9 as the main subject throughout. Include the Boxster S 3.4, Boxster Spyder and Boxster S Black Edition to explain similarities and differences. Keep Cayman comparisons to useful shared engineering; identify specifications that differ.
- **Shared components:** identify other model/year applications when supported by technical or parts evidence. Distinguish a shared design, the same original part and a later common replacement part; none alone proves that a complete assembly is interchangeable.
- Distinguish **model year**, **build date**, **announcement date**, **market launch** and **first registration**. A registration plate alone cannot establish which revision a car has.
- Use European kW and PS consistently, with Nm, litres, millimetres and kilograms. Add mph and imperial mpg for UK readers. Preserve a source's original horsepower convention when quoting it; do not silently equate PS and bhp.
- Label manual and PDK performance separately. Identify whether an acceleration figure requires Sport Chrono, and whether a weight is DIN, EC or a differently equipped vehicle.
- Treat UK and continental European standard equipment separately when they differ. Put US differences in short comparison boxes rather than applying US model-year labels to European launch dates.

## Part I The 2.9 litre Boxster through the years

### 1 The 2010 Boxster 2.9 in the 987.2 family

- Open with the 2010 2.9: identify its engine, drivetrain, body, cabin and standard equipment before introducing the wider range.
- Briefly trace the Boxster idea from the 986 to the original 987 and then the facelift.
- Explain the mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive layout and the relationship to the Cayman.
- Introduce the distinction between a new generation, a facelift and a running production change.
- Use the late 987.1 as the predecessor when explaining the facelift; keep the 2010 2.9 as the reference car for the book's comparisons.
- Use paired photographs to identify the facelift from the front, rear, cabin and engine specifications. Separate cosmetic clues from proof supplied by the VIN and build record.
- Explain why the RS 60 Spyder and Porsche Design Edition 2 belong in a predecessor sidebar rather than in the 987.2 special-model chapters. Porsche records these as 2008 derivatives. [Porsche Classic special models](https://www.porsche.com/uk/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/987b/987b-s/).

#### 2012 The change to the 981

- Begin with the final 987.2 2.9 specification and its documented differences from the 2010 reference.
- Explain the end of the 987.2 era and the arrival of its replacement, with separate dates for announcement and European sale.

- Keep the 981's specifications out of the final-987 tables. In particular, check engine capacity, output and steering type before accepting a brochure labelled “2012 Boxster”.
- Summarise documented final-year changes and carryovers. Where no annual equipment evidence is available, leave the exact detail unresolved rather than describing it as unchanged.

### 3 The 2010 Boxster 2.9 specification and options

- Collect the reference car's standard engine, transmission, chassis, body and cabin specifications in one place.
- Give manual and PDK configurations separate entries and distinguish standard equipment from factory options.
- Introduce the reference car's option codes, package dependencies and market differences, with links to the detailed component chapters.
- Follow the 2010 2.9 reference tables with compact comparisons for other years and derivatives.

#### A consistent format for every yearly chapter

- **The 2.9 in this year:** identify its specification, availability and position relative to the 2010 reference.
- **Mechanical changes:** engine, gearbox, differential, brakes, suspension and electronic control systems.
- **Exterior:** lighting, bumpers, wheels, badges, exhaust appearance, paint and roof colours.
- **Interior:** seats, trim, controls, instruments, entertainment and convenience equipment.
- **Options:** additions, deletions, package changes, prerequisites and changes to standard equipment.
- **Specifications:** comparable power, torque, weight, dimensions and performance figures.
- **Identification:** build details and photographs that establish which car is being described.
- **Carryovers:** a short explicit list, limited to details supported by the year's evidence.
- **Other models:** follow the 2.9 account with shared systems and differences on the S, Spyder and Black Edition, including their introduction dates and withdrawals.
- Finish with “What this means for the 2010 2.9 owner”: changes in use, driving experience and specification choice, without speculative resale claims.

## Part II The 2010 Boxster 2.9 component by component

Organise this part by systems, with individual components as subsections. Every main explanation and diagram starts with the **2010 Boxster 2.9**. Follow it with **2010 options**, **changes in other 2.9 model years**, and **shared applications and differences on other models**. Explain the main system before describing its smaller parts. Write for an interested reader with no engineering background, followed by optional technical detail.

### 4 Body structure and packaging

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** use this car's body shell, panel materials, engine access and luggage layout for the main cutaway and photographs.
- **Body shell and crash structure:** explain how the load-bearing shell provides stiffness and directs crash loads. Identify the bulkheads, sills, subframes, mounting points and panel materials.
- **Mid-engine packaging:** show where the engine, transmission, occupants, fuel tank and luggage sit; explain why concentrating mass near the centre affects the car's response to turning.
- **Exterior panels and access:** distinguish structural parts from removable panels. Show engine access, service access, front and rear lids, hinges, latches and seals.
- **Aerodynamics:** explain how airflow produces drag, lift and cooling. Cover the reference car's underbody, air intakes, ducts and rear spoiler.
- **2010 options and other years:** document the 2.9's styling choices, model identification deletion, paint and roof colours, then record changes against the 2010 reference. Use a labelled photo atlas.
- **Other models:** identify shared body components and explain differences in S details, Spyder panels/aerodynamics and Black Edition appearance.

### 5 Engine construction and valve gear

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** build the main explanation around this engine's construction and specifications; use it for the labelled engine drawing.
- **Flat-six operation:** illustrate the four-stroke cycle, opposed cylinders, crankshaft, connecting rods, pistons and combustion chambers.
- **Engine dimensions and output:** explain displacement, bore, stroke, compression ratio, torque and power using the 2.9's figures.
- **Cylinder heads and timing drive:** explain how chains keep crankshaft and camshafts synchronised, and how tensioners and guides control the chain path.
- **The intermediate-shaft change:** the new engines dispense with the earlier intermediate shaft. Explain the changed architecture without turning it into a claim that all engine failures were eliminated. [Porsche 2009 Technik Introduction, chain-drive section, printed p. 17 / viewer p. 23](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3550487/Porsche-Boxster-2009.html?page=23).
- **VarioCam Plus:** explain that altering intake-valve timing and lift changes how the engine breathes at different loads and speeds. Show the difference between timing and lift with two simple diagrams. [Porsche technical training, printed p. 18 / viewer p. 24](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3550487/Porsche-Boxster-2009.html?page=24).
- **2010 options and other years:** distinguish factory engine specification from optional equipment around it, then record documented changes to the 2.9 before and after 2010.
- **Other models:** compare the S, Spyder and Black Edition engines with the reference 2.9. Identify shared architecture and verified differences in capacity, parts and calibration; verify engine codes before asserting commonality.

### 6 Air fuel ignition and engine control

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** follow its port-injection system and engine management through the main explanation. Give direct injection its own comparison section.
- **Intake:** follow air through the filter, throttle and manifold to the cylinders. Explain how intake length and resonance can affect cylinder filling.
- **Fuel delivery:** follow fuel from the reference car's tank through its delivery system and injectors. Show how port injection sprays upstream of the inlet valves.
- **Ignition:** explain how plugs and coils ignite the charge, and how the control unit chooses the spark timing.
- **Sensors and electronic throttle:** show how pedal position, air measurement, temperature, oxygen and knock information contribute to engine control.
- **Crankcase ventilation:** explain blow-by gases, pressure control and separation of oil mist before gases return to the intake.
- **Tank ventilation and emissions controls:** explain why fuel vapour is contained rather than simply vented to the atmosphere.
- **2010 options and other years:** establish which features belong to the reference engine as standard, then record documented 2.9 component or software revisions.
- **Other models:** explain the direct-injection arrangement on the 3.4-litre models after the 2.9 system. Identify shared and different components using separate diagrams. The factory training manual treats the injection and management systems separately. [Porsche 2009 Technik Introduction, contents and engine sections](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3550487/Porsche-Boxster-2009.html).

### 7 Lubrication cooling and exhaust

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** draw its oil, coolant and exhaust circuits first, identifying transmission-dependent arrangements where applicable.
- **Oil circuit:** explain pressure-fed lubrication, scavenging, filtration, oil cooling and electronic level measurement. Show the actual integrated dry-sump layout rather than borrowing a diagram of an external-tank racing system.
- **Cooling circuit:** trace coolant between the engine, thermostat, pump, front radiators and expansion tank. Distinguish coolant radiators from air-conditioning condensers.
- **Airflow and heat management:** explain fan operation, ducting and why blocked heat exchangers affect heat rejection.
- **Exhaust:** follow gases through manifolds, catalysts and silencers. Explain oxygen-sensor feedback and the difference between sound control and emissions treatment.
- **Sports exhaust:** explain how an exhaust valve changes the gas path and sound. Distinguish the complete functional option from decorative tailpipes.
- **2010 options and other years:** explain the reference car's exhaust choices and record documented changes to the 2.9's lubrication, cooling and exhaust systems across the other years.
- **Other models:** identify shared circuits and differences in cooling or exhaust layouts on the 3.4-litre models; record any extra heat exchanger only where the relevant specification proves it.
- Technical reference: [Porsche 2009 training manual, oil, cooling and exhaust sections](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3550487/Porsche-Boxster-2009.html).

### 8 Manual transmission and clutch

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** use the manual-equipped reference car for the gearbox, clutch, flywheel and control diagrams. The following chapter covers its PDK alternative.
- **Clutch:** explain how friction connects and disconnects the running engine from the gearbox, including the hydraulic actuation.
- **Flywheel:** explain how rotational inertia and the dual-mass arrangement smooth engine pulses.
- **Gearbox:** illustrate gear ratios, synchronisers, shafts and the selector mechanism. Explain why a shorter ratio increases wheel torque for a given engine torque.
- **Driver controls:** connect the clutch pedal, gear lever and cables to the components they operate.
- **2010 options and other years:** verify short-shift choices for the 2010 2.9, then record changes in its ratios, final drive and components across the other years.
- **Other models:** identify shared transmission components and differences in S, Spyder and Black Edition gearboxes, clutches and ratios. Do not assume every manual variant uses identical ratios.

### 9 PDK transmission

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** explain PDK as a factory option on the reference car. Use the 2.9 application for the main operating and driver-control examples.
- **Basic operation:** show the two clutches and interleaved gear paths. The next likely ratio can be prepared while the current gear is transmitting power.
- **Control system:** explain how hydraulic actuation and electronic control coordinate clutch engagement and ratio selection.
- **Driver interface:** cover automatic mode, manual selection, the console selector, steering-wheel switches and paddles.
- **Sport functions:** explain the relationship between PDK, Sport Chrono, Sport Plus and Launch Control; use an equipment-dependency diagram.
- **2010 options and other years:** explain the 2.9's PDK control choices and Sport Chrono dependencies, then record documented changes from the 2010 arrangement. Keep steering-wheel choices separate from gearbox hardware or software changes.
- **Other models:** identify shared PDK architecture and verified differences in its applications or calibration on the S, Spyder and Black Edition.
- References for transmission architecture and controls: [Porsche 2009 training manual, PDK sections](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3550487/Porsche-Boxster-2009.html), [UK brochure, printed pp. 42–47](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf).

### 10 Differential driveshafts and mounts

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** show how the reference car transmits torque to the rear wheels and identify its standard differential, shafts, joints and mounts.
- **Final drive and open differential:** explain the final reduction and why the rear wheels need to rotate at different speeds in a corner.
- **Mechanical limited-slip differential:** explain how friction resists a difference in wheel speed, with a simple corner-exit example.
- **Electronic traction intervention:** distinguish braking a spinning wheel from physically fitting a locking differential.
- **Driveshafts and joints:** explain how torque reaches wheels that move with the suspension.
- **Mounts:** explain how rubber and other mounting elements control movement and isolate vibration.
- **2010 options and other years:** verify locking-differential availability for the reference 2.9 against transmission and wheel requirements, then record documented changes across its other years.
- **Other models:** identify shared assemblies and differences in the S and special models, including the Spyder's standard specification. Keep later parts supersessions separate from original factory changes.

### 11 Suspension and wheel geometry

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** begin with the reference car's standard suspension, component layout and geometry; explain optional damping afterwards.
- **Front and rear layouts:** identify struts, springs, dampers, arms, links, bushes, ball joints, anti-roll bars and bearings.
- **What the parts do:** springs support the car; dampers control oscillation; anti-roll bars link left and right suspension movement; joints and links guide the wheel.
- **Geometry:** explain camber, caster and toe using a wheel diagram. Connect geometry to steering response, tyre wear and stability without prescribing one universal alignment.
- **PASM:** explain electronically varied damping. The ordinary-model option combines it with a 10 mm lower ride height; it does not adjust ride height on demand. [Porsche launch brochure, printed pp. 54–55](https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/brochures5/26/2009%20987%20Boxster%20UK.pdf).
- **2010 options and other years:** compare the 2.9's standard suspension with its PASM option, then record documented changes relative to 2010. Separate aftermarket lowering from factory equipment.
- **Other models:** identify shared arms, links and other components, then compare the S and special-model suspension specifications. The Spyder brochure specifies a 20 mm reduction and a mechanical locking differential; explain these after the reference suspension and PASM option. [Porsche Spyder brochure, printed pp. 10–11](https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/brochures2/93/2010%20987%20Boxster%20Spyder.pdf).

### 12 Steering wheels and tyres

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** use the reference car's steering system and standard wheel/tyre fitment for the main explanation and dimensional drawings.
- **Steering:** explain the rack, pinion, tie rods and hydraulic assistance; distinguish mechanical ratio from assistance. The 987.2 technical data specifies hydraulic power assistance. [Porsche launch brochure, printed p. 127](https://porschecarshistory.com/wp-content/old/brochures5/26/2009%20987%20Boxster%20UK.pdf).
- **Wheels:** explain diameter, width, offset, bolt pattern, clearance and unsprung mass.
- **Tyres:** explain contact patches, sidewall dimensions, load and speed ratings, pressure and temperature.
- **Tyre-pressure monitoring:** explain the sensors, receiver and displayed warnings, including what is needed when wheels are changed.
- **2010 options and other years:** start the wheel catalogue with the 2.9's standard 17-inch fitment, then show its alternative wheels and pressure-monitoring option. Record changes against 2010, with front and rear dimensions.
- **Other models:** compare the S's 18-inch standard fitment and special-model wheel designs; identify shared steering or wheel components only where supported. [Porsche UK brochure, printed pp. 52–53 and 108–109](https://mrsportscars.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Porsche-Boxster-987-Gen-2-Sales-Brochure-UK-US-2010-2011.pdf).

### 13 Brakes and stability systems

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** make the reference car's braking hardware and standard stability functions the main subject; show its actual discs, calipers and control layout.
- **Hydraulic braking:** trace force from the pedal and booster through the master cylinder to calipers, pads and discs.
- **Heat:** explain the conversion of motion into heat, cooling, pad behaviour and brake-fluid boiling.
- **Parking brake:** explain its separate mechanical actuation and holding function.
- **ABS and PSM:** explain wheel-speed monitoring, yaw and steering information, individual braking and engine-torque intervention.
- **Hill-start assistance and brake assistance:** explain their distinct purposes and intervention conditions.
- **2010 options and other years:** distinguish the reference car's standard stability control from optional Sport Chrono settings, then document changes to the 2.9's hardware or functions in other years. Use the matching owner's manual for detailed operating behaviour.
- **Other models:** compare shared and different S/special-model brake hardware. ### 17 Audio navigation and communication

### 18 Occupant protection and system integration

- **2010 2.9 baseline:** begin with its protection systems and use a 2010 2.9 driving example to connect the systems explained throughout the book.

- **Sport Chrono:** explain it as a set of functions whose effects depend on the other equipment fitted. Include a matrix for manual versus PDK and relevant PCM/PASM dependencies.
- **2010 options and other years:** map relevant seat/equipment choices and Sport Chrono dependencies on the reference car, then record documented changes in other 2.9 years.
- **Other models:** close with shared safety systems and differences in how the S, Spyder and Black Edition combine equipment, always relating the effect back to the 2010 2.9.

## A repeatable component chapter template

Use these subsection titles in every component chapter, including where the system is shared across the range:

1. **The component on the 2010 Boxster 2.9:** locate it in a reference-car photograph or diagram, identify the standard configuration and name its parts.
2. **How it works:** explain its job and follow force, motion, fluid, airflow, heat or electrical information through the reference system. Describe what the driver sees, feels or hears.
3. **Options on the 2010 2.9:** show what buyers could change, what each option does and its prerequisites. Keep manual and PDK applicability explicit.
4. **Changes in other 2.9 model years:** compare 2009, 2011 and 2012 with the 2010 baseline, including supported carryovers and exact dates where known.
5. **Where else it is used and what differs:** identify shared applications and explain the S, Spyder and Black Edition differences. Add other Porsche applications only where the evidence supports them.
6. **Identifying the fitted version:** use build records, codes, controls and physical evidence to distinguish configurations on an actual car.
7. **Ageing and wear:** cover supported mechanisms briefly, using the reference car's system first; put variant-specific issues beside the relevant comparison.
8. **Sources:** put the exact document and page beside model-specific claims.

For example, the fuel-system chapter explains the 2010 2.9's port injection in full, then covers changes in other 2.9 years and compares the 3.4-litre direct-injection systems. The roof chapter explains the reference car's electric folding roof before comparing shared applications and the Spyder's manual arrangement.
